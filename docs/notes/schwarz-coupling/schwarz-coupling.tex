\title{The Constrained Dirichlet--Neumann Exchange\\
for Schwarz Coupling in Solid Dynamics}
\author{Alejandro Mota}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note specifies a Schwarz coupling for dynamic solid mechanics whose
discrete interface work vanishes at the converged state, so that the
coupled problem conserves the energy that its time integrators conserve.
The method is a Dirichlet--Neumann exchange between two nonoverlapping
subdomains, each advanced by its own Newmark scheme or by central
difference, on meshes that need not match at the interface and with time
steps that may differ by an integer ratio. It differs from the classical
Dirichlet--Neumann exchange in five respects: the Neumann side receives the
full dynamic (d'Alembert) reaction of its partner; both interface transfers
are built from one cross-mass matrix, so that the force transfer is the
transpose of the velocity transfer; the Dirichlet side receives the
velocity only and derives its displacement and acceleration from its own
time integrator; the iteration stops on the interface velocity jump and
the interface force residual; and the initial acceleration is computed on
the coupled system. At the fixed point the exchange solves the
velocity-constrained coupled problem of Gravouil and Combescure, of
Prakash and Hjelmstad, and of Connors, Owen, Kuberry, and Bochev, for which
the pseudo-energy (the energy functional applied to the velocity and the
acceleration) is conserved exactly for linear elasticity, for any pair of
Newmark schemes, for any mesh ratio, and, with the coarse-step side as the
Dirichlet side, for any integer step ratio. The note states the formulation
and its proof, the algorithm in the form needed for an implementation in a
production code (interface operators, the exchange, the stopping
criterion, the coupled initial acceleration, subcycling, the assignment of
the Dirichlet role, relaxation by Aitken or Anderson acceleration, and a
direct interface solve for two explicit subdomains), and its verification
on a cantilever beam and on nested cylinders: the matched energy is
constant to the Schwarz tolerance over $10^4$ steps in every configuration
measured, and to roundoff with the direct solve, while the classical
exchange inverts an element within $1$\,ms with implicit subdomains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and requirements}
\label{sec:problem}

A solid body $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is decomposed into two
subdomains $\Omega_D$ and $\Omega_N$ that share an interface
$\Gamma = \partial\Omega_D \cap \partial\Omega_N$ and are integrated in time
independently, each with its own mesh, its own time integrator, and its own
time step. The subdomains are coupled by a Schwarz iteration: within each
time window the two subdomains are solved in turn, exchanging interface
data, until the data converge, following Mota, Tezaur, and
Phlipot~\cite{MotaTezaurPhlipot2022}. The coupling must satisfy the
following requirements.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Energy conservation.} At the converged state the coupling does
  no work on the system: the coupled problem conserves the quantity that
  the subdomain integrators conserve, to the tolerance of the Schwarz
  iteration.
\item \emph{Nonconforming meshes.} The two meshes need not share nodes on
  $\Gamma$.
\item \emph{Heterogeneous integrators and time steps.} Each subdomain may use
  an implicit Newmark scheme or central difference, and the time steps may
  differ by an integer ratio.
\item \emph{Partitioned solution.} Each subdomain is advanced by its own
  solver; the coupling requires of a subdomain only operations that a
  structural dynamics code already provides (Sec.~\ref{sec:integration}).
\end{enumerate}
The classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange satisfies the last three
requirements but not the first: with implicit subdomains on the beam of
Sec.~\ref{sec:verification} it inverts an element within $1$\,ms,
and with explicit subdomains it conserves the energy only to $2$ to $4\%$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}). The constrained exchange of this note satisfies
all four.

\section{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Semi-discrete equations.} After finite element discretization
in space, subdomain $k \in \{D, N\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  ~M_k\, ~a_k + ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k(~u_k)
  = ~f^{\mathrm{ext}}_k + ~C_k^T ~\lambda_k ,
  \label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
with $~u_k$, $~v_k$, $~a_k$ the nodal displacement, velocity, and
acceleration, $~M_k$ the mass matrix (lumped for central difference),
$~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k$ the internal force, $~f^{\mathrm{ext}}_k$ the external
force, $~C_k$ the Boolean restriction of the nodal vector to the interface
nodes, and $~\lambda_k$ the interface force acting on subdomain $k$. For
linear elasticity $~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k = ~K_k ~u_k$.

\paragraph{Time integrators.} Subdomain $k$ is advanced by the Newmark
scheme with parameters $(\beta_k, \gamma_k)$ and step $\Delta t_k$:
\begin{equation}
  ~u^{n+1} = ~u^{n} + \Delta t\, ~v^{n}
    + \Delta t^2 \bigl[(\tfrac12 - \beta)\, ~a^{n} + \beta\, ~a^{n+1}\bigr],
  \qquad
  ~v^{n+1} = ~v^{n} + \Delta t \bigl[(1 - \gamma)\, ~a^{n} + \gamma\, ~a^{n+1}\bigr].
  \label{eq:newmark}
\end{equation}
The trapezoidal rule ($\beta = 1/4$, $\gamma = 1/2$, implicit, denoted I)
and central difference ($\beta = 0$, $\gamma = 1/2$, explicit with a lumped
mass, denoted E) are the two schemes used below. With the predictors
$~u^{\mathrm{pred}} = ~u^n + \Delta t\, ~v^n + (\tfrac12 - \beta)\Delta t^2 ~a^n$
and $~v^{\mathrm{pred}} = ~v^n + (1-\gamma)\Delta t\, ~a^n$, the scheme gives
\begin{equation}
  ~a^{n+1} = \frac{~v^{n+1} - ~v^{\mathrm{pred}}}{\gamma\, \Delta t},
  \qquad
  ~u^{n+1} = ~u^{\mathrm{pred}} + \beta\, \Delta t^2\, ~a^{n+1},
  \label{eq:newmark-from-v}
\end{equation}
so a prescribed end-of-step velocity determines the acceleration and the
displacement of the step.

\paragraph{Roles.} The \emph{Dirichlet side} $D$ receives a kinematic datum
on its interface nodes; the \emph{Neumann side} $N$ receives a force. A
pair of integrators is written with the integrator of one named subdomain
first (II, IE, EI, EE).

\paragraph{Interface operators.} Let $\varphi^D_m$ and $\varphi^N_n$ be the
interface traces of the nodal basis functions of the two sides. The
cross-mass matrix and the boundary mass matrices are
\begin{equation}
  B_{mn} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^D_m\, \varphi^N_n \,\dd S, \qquad
  (~W_D)_{ml} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^D_m\, \varphi^D_l \,\dd S, \qquad
  (~W_N)_{nl} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^N_n\, \varphi^N_l \,\dd S,
  \label{eq:cross-mass}
\end{equation}
assembled once on the reference configuration, per Cartesian component.
The kinematic transfer to the Dirichlet side and its transpose are
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_D = ~W_D^{-1} ~B, \qquad \Pi_D^T = ~B^T ~W_D^{-1} .
  \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
$\Pi_D$ maps the interface values of side $N$ to the $L^2$ projection of
their trace onto the trace space of side $D$; it reproduces constants when
$~B\,\mathbf{1} = ~W_D \mathbf{1}$, that is, when the quadrature of $~B$ is
exact on products of the two trace spaces.

\paragraph{Windows.} The Schwarz controller advances in \emph{stops}. Within
a stop each subdomain advances to the stop end in its own steps; the
\emph{Schwarz iteration} repeats a solve of each subdomain with interface
data exchanged between the solves until the data converge, and the stop is
then committed. A stop that spans $m$ steps of a subdomain is a
\emph{window} of $m$ substeps for that subdomain.

\paragraph{Energies.} The energy is
\begin{equation}
  E = \sum_k \Bigl(\tfrac12\, ~v_k^T ~M_k\, ~v_k + \mathrm{SE}(~u_k)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{SE}$ the strain energy. For central difference the kinetic
term is evaluated in the staggered form
$\tfrac12\, ~v^T ~M ~v - \tfrac{\Delta t^2}{8}\, ~a^T ~M ~a$, which is the
kinetic part of the quadratic that the scheme conserves exactly on a linear
problem; the consistent-mass kinetic energy at integer steps misrepresents
the short-wavelength content of a sharp front by several percent. For
linear elasticity the pseudo-energy is
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{E} = \sum_k \Bigl(\tfrac12\, ~a_k^T ~S_k\, ~a_k
             + \tfrac12\, ~v_k^T ~K_k\, ~v_k\Bigr),
  \qquad
  ~S_k = ~M_k + \Bigl(\beta_k - \frac{\gamma_k}{2}\Bigr)\Delta t_k^2\, ~K_k ,
  \label{eq:pseudo-energy}
\end{equation}
the energy functional applied to the velocity and the acceleration, which
is the energy of the time-differentiated system
$~M \dot{~a} + ~K ~v = \dot{~f}$; its unit is J/s$^2$. $~S_k$ is $~M_k$ for
the trapezoidal rule and $~M_k - \tfrac14 \Delta t_k^2 ~K_k$ for central
difference, positive definite exactly below the stability limit of central
difference. Relative to $E$, $\tilde{E}$ weights each vibration mode by the
square of its frequency.

\section{The constrained coupled problem}
\label{sec:constrained}

\subsection{Formulation}

The coupled problem consists of \eqref{eq:motion} on both sides with one
interface force acting in opposite directions,
\begin{equation}
  ~\lambda_D = ~\lambda, \qquad ~\lambda_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda ,
  \label{eq:action-reaction}
\end{equation}
and the velocity constraint at the end of every window,
\begin{equation}
  ~v_D = \Pi_D\, ~v_N \quad \text{on } \Gamma .
  \label{eq:velocity-constraint}
\end{equation}
The interface force $~\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier of
\eqref{eq:velocity-constraint}. On matching meshes $\Pi_D$ is the identity
on the interface nodes and the formulation is that of Gravouil and
Combescure~\cite{GravouilCombescure2001}, a dual Schur formulation with
velocity continuity. Prakash and Hjelmstad~\cite{PrakashHjelmstad2004}
impose the constraint only at the end of a coarse step, with the force on
the fine side interpolated linearly in time; Connors, Owen, Kuberry, and
Bochev~\cite{ConnorsEtAl2024} cast the same structure as a
discontinuous-Galerkin-in-time multirate framework whose lowest flux order
is this scheme.

The force on the interface rows of side $D$ is, by \eqref{eq:motion}, its
d'Alembert reaction
\begin{equation}
  ~r_D = ~C_D \bigl( ~M_D\, ~a_D + ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_D - ~f^{\mathrm{body}}_D
        - ~f^{\mathrm{bdry}}_D \bigr) = ~\lambda ,
  \label{eq:reaction}
\end{equation}
with $~f^{\mathrm{body}}_D$ the body force and $~f^{\mathrm{bdry}}_D$ the
other boundary loads acting on the interface rows. The reaction includes
the inertia of the interface nodes; the static reaction
$-~f^{\mathrm{int}}$ omits a term of the size of the reaction itself
(Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}).

\subsection{The energy identity}
\label{sec:identity}

Connors et al.\ (their Eq.~(102)) give the energy balance of one side. For
side $k$ advanced by $m_k$ Newmark steps with a linear stiffness, an
interface force $~f_k$ on its interface rows, and no other loads,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{E}_{k}^{m_k} - \tilde{E}_{k}^{0}
  + \Bigl(\gamma_k - \tfrac12\Bigr) \sum_{n=1}^{m_k}
    (\Delta ~a_k^n)^T ~S_k\, \Delta ~a_k^n
  = \frac{1}{\Delta t_k} \sum_{n=1}^{m_k}
    (\Delta ~v_k^n)^T ~C_k^T\, \Delta ~f_k^n ,
  \label{eq:per-side}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta(\cdot)^n = (\cdot)^n - (\cdot)^{n-1}$ and $\tilde{E}_k$ the
term of side $k$ in \eqref{eq:pseudo-energy}. With $\gamma_k = 1/2$ the
second term vanishes.

\begin{proposition}[Equal steps]
\label{prop:equal}
Let both sides take one step $\Delta t$ per window, with $\gamma = 1/2$ on
both, a linear stiffness, no loads on the interface rows other than the
interface force, $~f_D = ~\lambda$ and $~f_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda$, and
$~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ at every step. Then $\tilde{E}_D + \tilde{E}_N$ is
constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:per-side} the change over one step is
$\Delta t^{-1}\bigl[(\Delta ~v_D)^T \Delta~\lambda
- (\Delta ~v_N)^T \Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda\bigr]
= \Delta t^{-1} (\Delta ~v_D - \Pi_D \Delta ~v_N)^T \Delta~\lambda = 0$.
\end{proof}

The cancellation needs only the transposed force transfer and the
one-sided constraint; it holds for any cross-mass matrix $~B$, so for any
mesh ratio, and for any pair of Newmark schemes with $\gamma = 1/2$. It is
the interface work identity of Gravouil and Combescure (their Eq.~(49))
written with $\Pi_D$ in place of the Boolean interface map. Identical
integrators at equal steps conserve $E$ as well: the constraint and the
common Newmark relations then give $~u_D = \Pi_D ~u_N$ up to the initial
data, and the interface power $~\lambda^T (~v_D - \Pi_D ~v_N)$ vanishes at
the end of every step.

\begin{proposition}[Different steps]
\label{prop:subcycled}
Let side $D$ take one step $\Delta T$ per window and side $N$ take $m$
steps $\Delta T/m$, under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:equal}
except that $~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ holds only at the two ends of the window,
and let the force on $N$ at its substep $j$ be the linear interpolation
\begin{equation}
  ~f_N^{\,j} = -\Pi_D^T \Bigl[\bigl(1 - \tfrac{j}{m}\bigr)~\lambda^0
             + \tfrac{j}{m}\, ~\lambda^1\Bigr], \qquad j = 0, \dots, m,
  \label{eq:interpolated-force}
\end{equation}
of the forces $~\lambda^0$ and $~\lambda^1$ on $D$ at the window ends. Then
$\tilde{E}_D + \tilde{E}_N$ is constant over the window.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Delta ~f_N^j = -\Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda / m$ for every substep, with
$\Delta~\lambda = ~\lambda^1 - ~\lambda^0$, so the right side of
\eqref{eq:per-side} for $N$ is
$\frac{m}{\Delta T}\sum_{j=1}^{m} (\Delta ~v_N^j)^T(-\Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda/m)
= -\Delta T^{-1}\bigl(\Pi_D(~v_N^m - ~v_N^0)\bigr)^T \Delta~\lambda$, and
that of $D$ is $\Delta T^{-1}(~v_D^1 - ~v_D^0)^T \Delta~\lambda$. The two
cancel when $~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ at both window ends.
\end{proof}

This is Eq.~(103) of Connors et al.\ with $\Pi_D$ in place of their Boolean
map, their condition (108) at flux order $r = 1$, and Eq.~(70) of Prakash
and Hjelmstad. The force on $N$ at the window start must be the converged
force of the previous window, and the loads on the interface rows of $D$
must be constant within the window, because the reaction
\eqref{eq:reaction} is formed from their current value.

\paragraph{The coarse step must be the Dirichlet side.} The same sums
cancel when the fine-step side is the Dirichlet side and meets the
linearly interpolated coarse velocity at every substep, the arrangement of
Gravouil and Combescure. That arrangement conserves $\tilde{E}$ but lacks
the flux projection property of Connors et al.\ (their Eq.~(80)): the
impulse that the fine side receives over the window, the trapezoidal sum of
its reactions at every substep, differs from the impulse that the coarse
side receives, the trapezoid of the two end values. The interface impulse
residual of a window,
\begin{equation}
  ~\rho = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{\Delta T}{2m}\bigl(~f_N^{\,j-1} + ~f_N^{\,j}\bigr)
        + \Pi_D^T\, \frac{\Delta T}{2}\bigl(~\lambda^0 + ~\lambda^1\bigr),
  \label{eq:impulse}
\end{equation}
vanishes identically in the coarse-step arrangement. In the fine-step
arrangement it is of the order of the impulse itself, and the energy $E$
varies by $12$ to $51\%$ over the period of the first bending mode of the
beam while $\tilde{E}$ is conserved (Sec.~\ref{sec:verify-subcycled}).
$\tilde{E}$ weights the lowest mode least, so it does not detect the
variation.

\paragraph{Scope.} Propositions~\ref{prop:equal} and~\ref{prop:subcycled}
hold for a linear stiffness. For a nonlinear material the exchange still
closes the interface exactly and does no interface work at the fixed point;
the energy then varies by the time-step error of the subdomain integrators
themselves. For central difference that error is of order $\Delta t^2$ and
does not grow: the scheme conserves a modified energy that differs from $E$
by a state-dependent term of order $\Delta t^2$
\cite{HairerLubichWanner2006}. Conservation of $E$ to roundoff for a
nonlinear material requires an energy-momentum
integrator~\cite{SimoTarnow1992,Gonzalez1996} in each subdomain and a
midpoint velocity constraint in the exchange, which this note does not
treat.

\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Setup}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Assign the Dirichlet role (Sec.~\ref{sec:roles}).
\item Assemble $~B$, $~W_D$, and $~W_N$ of \eqref{eq:cross-mass} on the
  reference configuration with the facet quadrature of the finer side, and
  form $\Pi_D$ of \eqref{eq:projector}. Check that $\Pi_D$ reproduces
  constants (the partition-of-unity error $\max |\Pi_D \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}|$
  is at roundoff when the quadrature is exact). Only $\Pi_D$ and its
  transpose enter the exchange; the projection in the other direction is
  never formed, which matters on curved or non-nested interfaces where it
  is inaccurate.
\item Compute the initial acceleration on the coupled system
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:initial}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{One window}
\label{sec:window}

Given the converged state at the window start and the force $~\lambda^0$ of
the previous window:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Initialize the datum $~v_D^\ast$ of the Dirichlet side, for example
  with the projected partner velocity of the previous window.
\item Repeat until the stopping criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping} holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item Advance side $D$ over the window with $~v_D = ~v_D^\ast$ imposed on
    its interface nodes at the window end, the interface acceleration and
    displacement from \eqref{eq:newmark-from-v}, and form its reaction
    $~\lambda^1 = ~r_D$ by \eqref{eq:reaction}.
  \item Advance side $N$ over its substeps with the force
    \eqref{eq:interpolated-force} on its interface nodes (at equal steps,
    $~f_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda^1$).
  \item Form the new datum $\Pi_D ~v_N$ at the window end and relax it
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}) into $~v_D^\ast$.
  \end{enumerate}
\item Commit the window and keep $~\lambda^1$ as the next $~\lambda^0$.
\end{enumerate}
Each Schwarz iteration restores both sides to the state at the window
start before it advances them. With the displacement constraint instead of
the velocity constraint (trapezoidal pairs only) the datum is
$\Pi_D ~u_N$ and the velocity and acceleration follow from the Newmark
relations; the velocity constraint is the general choice.

\subsection{Stopping criterion}
\label{sec:stopping}

The iteration stops when both the one-sided relative velocity jump and the
relative force residual fall below a tolerance $\epsilon$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\norm{~v_D - \Pi_D ~v_N}_{~W_D}}{\norm{~v_D}_{~W_D}} < \epsilon,
  \qquad
  \frac{\norm{~f_N + \Pi_D^T ~r_D}_{~W_N^{-1}}}{\norm{\Pi_D^T ~r_D}_{~W_N^{-1}}} < \epsilon,
  \label{eq:stopping}
\end{equation}
the force residual taken at every substep of the window. The norms are the
discrete $L^2$ norms of the trace and of its dual. An absolute floor, if
any, must be given in the units of the constrained quantity; a
displacement tolerance divided by the step is not a valid velocity floor
(on the cylinders of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification} it amounts to $200$\,m/s
and stops the iteration after one pass). A stop at which the residual
stalls above $\epsilon$ for three consecutive iterations without falling
$5\%$ below its smallest value may be accepted with a warning; the
residual is not monotone under Aitken or Anderson acceleration, so a single
non-decreasing iteration is not a stall. Every stopping tolerance leaves a
drift of the conserved quantity of the order of $\epsilon$ per window,
accumulated over the windows of the run (Sec.~\ref{sec:verify-relaxation}).

\subsection{Coupled initial acceleration}
\label{sec:initial}

The initial accelerations must satisfy the coupled problem at $t = 0$,
including the acceleration constraint $~a_D = \Pi_D ~a_N$, or the first
step changes $\tilde{E}$ by $0.2$ to $0.9\%$ and, for an explicit pair, the
difference of the two interface accelerations alternates in sign at every
step without decaying. The coupled initial acceleration is a Schwarz
iteration at $t = 0$ in which $D$ imposes the projected partner
acceleration, $N$ receives the d'Alembert reaction of $D$, and each side
solves for its acceleration with the interface rows included; it converges
in $2$ iterations on conforming meshes with identical integrators, $16$ to
$24$ on nonconforming meshes, and about $30$ for mixed pairs at a fixed
relaxation factor of $0.5$, and in $2$ to $9$ with Anderson acceleration.
For two explicit sides the direct solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:direct} gives it
in one step.

\subsection{Assignment of the Dirichlet role}
\label{sec:roles}

The linearized gain of one Schwarz iteration at fixed relaxation is
\begin{equation}
  ~G = -\,\Pi_D\, ~H_N\, \Pi_D^T\, ~S_D ,
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
with $~H_N = ~C_N \tilde{~M}_N^{-1} ~C_N^T$ the interface flexibility of $N$
($\tilde{~M} = ~M + \beta \Delta t^2 ~K$, the lumped mass for central
difference) and $~S_D$ the interface dynamic stiffness of $D$. A fixed
relaxation factor $\theta$ converges if and only if the spectral radius of
$(1 - \theta)~I + \theta ~G$ is below one; for a real spectrum in $[a, b]$
the best factor is $\theta^\ast = 2/((1-a) + (1-b))$ with contraction
$(b - a)/((1-a) + (1-b))$. Table~\ref{tab:dn-gain} lists the computed
eigenvalues on the beam. The rules that follow, in order of precedence:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item With different time steps, the coarse-step side is the Dirichlet side
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:subcycled} and the impulse residual).
\item With different integrators, the implicit side is the Dirichlet side:
  with the explicit side as the Dirichlet side the eigenvalues reach
  $-13.3$ and every factor $\theta \ge 0.5$ diverges.
\item With the same integrator on a nonconforming interface, the finer mesh
  is the Dirichlet side: the eigenvalues then lie in $[g, 0]$ with
  $|g| \le 1$; with the coarser mesh as the Dirichlet side they reach $-8$
  for an explicit pair.
\end{enumerate}
When rules 1 and 3 conflict (a finer mesh with a finer step), giving the
finer mesh the coarser step satisfies both where the stability limit of an
explicit side allows it. With Anderson acceleration every assignment
measured converges, so the rules then set the cost, not the feasibility.

\subsection{Relaxation and acceleration}
\label{sec:relaxation}

\paragraph{Aitken.} The Aitken factor must be formed from the projected
interface trace $\Pi_D ~v_N$ that $D$ receives. Formed from the whole
partner field it tends to $1$: the interior values do not enter the
exchange, follow a map of zero gain, and dominate the residual, and at
$\theta = 1$ the interface map, of gain near $-1$, does not contract.

\paragraph{Anderson.} Anderson acceleration~\cite{WalkerNi2011}, in the
interface quasi-Newton form of Degroote, Bathe, and
Vierendeels~\cite{DegrooteBatheVierendeels2009}, combines the last $m$
iterates. With $~x_k$ the datum $D$ used, $~f_k = G(~x_k) - ~x_k$ the
residual, $~t_k$ its projected trace, and $\Delta ~T$, $\Delta ~X$,
$\Delta ~F$ the differences of the last $m + 1$ iterates,
\begin{equation}
  ~\gamma = \arg\min \norm{~t_k - \Delta ~T ~\gamma}, \qquad
  ~x_{k+1} = ~x_k + \beta\, ~f_k - (\Delta ~X + \beta\, \Delta ~F)\, ~\gamma ,
  \label{eq:anderson}
\end{equation}
with mixing parameter $\beta$, the least-squares problem solved by QR with
the oldest column dropped while the condition number exceeds $10^{10}$. The
first iteration of a window has no history and is the fixed relaxation
$~x_1 = ~x_0 + \beta ~f_0$; for $k \ge 1$ the term $\beta ~f_k$ is damped by
$\beta$ and the history term corrects it. With $\beta = 1$ the update is the
undamped quasi-Newton step. On the beam ($1$\,ms, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$),
$\beta = 0.5$ needs $7.0$, $23.1$, and $7.9$ iterations per window on the
implicit pair, on the mixed pair with the explicit side as the Dirichlet
side, and on the explicit pair at ratio $1{:}0.5$ with $4{:}1$ steps and the
coarse mesh as the Dirichlet side, against $8.9$, $28.7$, and $8.8$ at
$\beta = 1$, with the same energies; $\beta = 0.5$ is the recommended
value and the default in Norma. For a linear interface map with $\beta = 1$ and a depth at least
the iteration count, the iterate equals the map applied to the GMRES iterate
of the same index (Walker and Ni, Theorem~2.2). The first iteration of a
window has no history and is not entered in it. A depth of $10$ covers
every case measured; a depth of $5$ is too short for an explicit Dirichlet
side, whose window needs $12$ to $16$ iterations.

\paragraph{State.} The relaxation state is kept per interface pair and per
substep time, and cleared at every stop. A state shared across substeps
blends iterates of different times, which acts as a causal filter on the
exchanged datum and moves the converged solution. Aitken and Anderson
acceleration apply to stops of one substep; a stop that spans several
substeps of the relaxed side uses a fixed factor.

\subsection{Direct interface solve for two explicit subdomains}
\label{sec:direct}

For central difference on both sides at equal steps the iteration can be
replaced by one solve per step. Central difference computes $~u^{n+1}$
before the acceleration, and the internal force at $~u^{n+1}$ does not
depend on the interface force. Advancing both sides with free interface
rows and no interface force gives the velocities $~v^{\mathrm{free}}$; the
interface force adds $\gamma \Delta t\, ~m_D^{-1}~\lambda$ to the velocity
of $D$ and $-\gamma\Delta t\, ~m_N^{-1}\Pi_D^T ~\lambda$ to that of $N$, with
$~m_D$, $~m_N$ the lumped masses of the interface rows. The velocity
constraint then gives, per Cartesian component,
\begin{equation}
  \gamma \Delta t \bigl(~m_D^{-1} + \Pi_D\, ~m_N^{-1}\, \Pi_D^T\bigr)
  ~\lambda = \Pi_D ~v_N^{\mathrm{free}} - ~v_D^{\mathrm{free}} .
  \label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
The matrix is symmetric positive definite, of the size of the Dirichlet
trace, and constant while the lumped masses and $\Pi_D$ are; rows held by
other Dirichlet conditions take a zero inverse mass. It is the interface
operator $~H = \sum_k \gamma_k \Delta t_k ~C_k \tilde{~M}_k^{-1} ~C_k^T$ of
Gravouil and Combescure for two lumped-mass sides, written on the Dirichlet
trace. Factor it once (a dense Cholesky factorization of $3920$ unknowns
per component on the cylinders takes $0.7$\,s), and at every step advance
both sides, solve \eqref{eq:direct}, and correct the interface
accelerations and velocities. The solve is exact for nonlinear materials,
because the internal force is evaluated at the predicted displacement.
The same matrix gives the coupled initial acceleration from
$~a_D = \Pi_D ~a_N$.

\subsection{Requirements on a subdomain code}
\label{sec:integration}

The exchange requires of each subdomain code, beyond a standard step:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item a step with a prescribed velocity on a set of boundary nodes, with
  the displacement and acceleration of those nodes from
  \eqref{eq:newmark-from-v} (the Dirichlet side);
\item a step with a prescribed nodal force on a set of boundary nodes,
  constant or linear in time over the window (the Neumann side);
\item the nodal d'Alembert reaction \eqref{eq:reaction} on a set of
  boundary nodes after a step;
\item saving and restoring the state at the window start;
\item the boundary mass matrix and the facet quadrature of its interface,
  to assemble $~B$ (performed once, by either code or by the coupler).
\end{enumerate}
An implicit code provides items 1 to 3 as a Newton solve at fixed boundary
data; an explicit code provides them as a division by the lumped mass. The
coupler itself holds $\Pi_D$, the relaxation state, and, for two explicit
sides, the factorization of \eqref{eq:direct}.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

\subsection{Benchmarks}

\paragraph{Cantilever beam.} A linear elastic bar ($E = 6.895$\,GPa,
$\nu = 0.25$, $\rho = 2768$\,kg/m$^3$; $254 \times 25.4 \times 12.7$\,mm),
clamped at $x = 0$ and released at rest from the bent shape
$u_y = 0.3937\,x^2$ (tip deflection $25.4$\,mm), with initial energy
$1506$\,J and a first bending period of about $10$\,ms. It is split at
$x = 127$\,mm into a clamped part and a free part. The free part is meshed
with HEX8 elements of $6.35$\,mm; the clamped part at $6.35$\,mm
(conforming), $8.47$\,mm (ratio $1{:}0.75$, facets not nested), or
$12.7$\,mm (ratio $1{:}0.5$, nested). Stops of $1\,\mu$s (or $0.5\,\mu$s
where stated), $10$\,ms. The first letter of an integrator pair is the
clamped part.

\paragraph{Nested cylinders.} A steel shell (outer radius $88.9$\,mm,
height $330$\,mm), a soft filler of one fiftieth of the shell's wave
impedance, and an offset core, all neohookean, launched at $-35$\,m/s
against a support that fixes the bottom face in $z$. The interface is a
closed surface inside the filler around the core (a lateral surface and two
caps); the inner mesh is $5.1$\,mm and the outer $6.35$\,mm (ratio
$1{:}0.8$, not nested), $17$ to $26$ thousand elements per subdomain.
Central difference on both sides at a Courant number of $0.5$, stops of
$0.5\,\mu$s, $2$\,ms. A linear elastic version with the same meshes
(filler $E = 0.214$\,GPa, $\nu = 0.494$) moves at $-5$\,m/s for $1$\,ms.
The inner side is the Dirichlet side.

\paragraph{Ground truth.} The undecomposed beam keeps $E$ and $\tilde{E}$
within $1.7\times 10^{-13}$ over $10$\,ms with the trapezoidal rule and
within $2.7\times 10^{-14}$ with central difference; the trapezoidal value
requires a direct linear solver and a tight tolerance on the
initial-acceleration solve.

\subsection{The classical exchange}
\label{sec:baseline}

The classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange departs from the constrained
problem in the five respects listed in the abstract: it transmits the
static reaction $-~f^{\mathrm{int}}$, builds its two transfers
independently, imposes displacement, velocity, and acceleration together,
stops on the displacement update of the whole domain, and computes the
initial acceleration per subdomain with the interface held fixed. The
relative difference between the force it applies on the Neumann side and
the transposed d'Alembert reaction of the Dirichlet side, measured in the
$~W_N^{-1}$ norm at the end of every stop, has the median $0.83$ (maximum
$4.2$) with explicit subdomains and $1.47$ (maximum $4.6$) with implicit
ones; under the constrained exchange it is below $4\times 10^{-16}$. On the
beam the classical exchange inverts an element within $1$\,ms with implicit
subdomains on every mesh ratio and with $4{:}1$ steps, and with explicit
subdomains the energy varies by $2.6$ to $4.3\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:beam}). On
the neohookean cylinders it stays within $3\%$ over $2$\,ms with explicit
subdomains.

The overlap decomposition admits no construction of this kind: its two
sides exchange data on two different surfaces, so there is no shared
interface on which the two works can cancel. Robin and impedance
transmission conditions on a shared interface were measured in the
previous version of this note; none conserved on nonconforming meshes,
because the transmission condition, not only the Schwarz iteration,
defines their fixed point. A Robin condition assembled from the same
reaction and the same one-sided constraint has the fixed point of the
constrained problem and could serve as a solver of it.

\section{Limitations and open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Inexact Schwarz iteration.} Every iterated run stops at a
  tolerance, and the stopping error accumulates: the conserved quantity
  drifts by about $10^{-10}$ over $10^4$ stops at $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$, and
  by about $10^{-9}$ with the fine-step side as the Dirichlet side. How the
  drift scales with $\epsilon$ and with the number of windows, and whether
  the criterion \eqref{eq:stopping} needs refinement, has not been
  measured.
\item \emph{Mixed pair with an explicit coarse-step side.} The coarse-step
  side must be the Dirichlet side and an explicit Dirichlet side has a gain
  down to $-13$; Anderson acceleration converges at equal steps in this
  configuration but has not been measured with subcycling.
\item \emph{Cause of the energy swing with the fine-step side as the
  Dirichlet side.} The swing coincides with an impulse residual of the
  order of the impulse; that the residual causes it has not been tested
  separately.
\item \emph{Nonlinear materials.} Conservation of $E$ to roundoff needs an
  energy-momentum integrator and a midpoint velocity constraint.
\item \emph{Higher flux order and window solves.} Flux order $r \ge 2$ in
  the sense of Connors et al.\ and a direct interface solve over a
  subcycled window are not needed for conservation; whether they reduce
  the cost has not been measured.
\item \emph{Implicit subdomains on the cylinders} and more than two
  subdomains have not been run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implementation in Norma}
\label{sec:norma}

The exchange is a \code{Schwarz DN nonoverlap} condition with
\code{constrained: true} on both sides of a pair and
\code{constraint: velocity} (or \code{displacement} for trapezoidal pairs);
\code{default BC type} assigns the Dirichlet and Neumann roles;
\code{interface solve: direct} selects the direct solve for two explicit
sides at equal steps. The top-level input sets the relative tolerance
$\epsilon$, \code{relaxation: anderson} with \code{anderson depth} and
\code{relaxation parameter} (the mixing parameter $\beta$, default $0.5$ under
Anderson acceleration and $1$ otherwise), or
\code{relaxation: aitken recursive}; \code{constraint absolute tolerance}
sets an absolute floor in the units of the constrained quantity (default
$0$), and \code{stalled interface jump action} selects what a stalled stop
does. \code{blended energy output: true} writes, per stop, the energy $E$
(\code{total\_energy}), the pseudo-energy $\tilde{E}$
(\code{pseudo\_energy\_total} and per subdomain; NaN for nonlinear
materials), the interface displacement and velocity jumps, and, for
subcycled pairs, the impulse residual \eqref{eq:impulse}. The keys are
documented in \code{docs/src/reference/multidomain.md}. The implementation
is in \code{src/schwarz.jl}, \code{src/simulation.jl}, and
\code{src/overlap\_energy.jl}; tests 135 (pseudo-energy) and 136
(constrained exchange, including the direct solve, subcycling, Aitken and
Anderson acceleration, and the equivalence of Anderson acceleration with
GMRES on a probed interface map), and 137 (windowed stops). Examples:
\code{examples/nonoverlap/dynamic-same-step/cantilever-dn} (the beam, from
which test 136 builds its constrained pairs),
\code{examples/nonoverlap/dynamic-same-step/cantilever-dn-nonconforming}
(the beam at mesh ratio $1{:}0.5$), and
\code{examples/jmp/concentric-cylinders-linear}. The parameter study
\code{studies/dynamic-stability} measures the effect of each factor
(exchange, mesh ratio, tolerance, time steps, solver, refinement) on the
beam.
