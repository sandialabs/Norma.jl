\title{Schwarz Coupling for Solid Mechanics in Norma:\\
Formulation, Algorithms, and Verification}
\author{Alejandro Mota}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note specifies the Schwarz alternating method as implemented in
Norma for the coupling of finite element subdomains in solid mechanics,
in the form needed to implement the same methods in another code. A
multidomain simulation advances in controller steps, called stops; within
a stop each subdomain is advanced by its own time integrator, with its own
mesh and time step, and the subdomains are solved in turn with interface
data exchanged between the solves until the data converge. The note
states the controller (stops, substeps, the time interpolation of partner
data, the stopping criteria, the relaxation and acceleration of the
iteration, and the control of the Newton tolerance of the subdomain
solves), the interface transfer operators (interpolation in a partner
mesh and $L^2$ projections built from boundary mass matrices), and the
five transmission conditions in the code: the overlapping Dirichlet
coupling, the classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange on a shared interface,
the matching Dirichlet--Neumann exchange, the Robin--Robin condition, and
the contact coupling. The matching exchange is treated in the most
detail, because it is the only one whose fixed point conserves the energy
of a dynamic problem: its Dirichlet side matches one kinematic quantity
of the partner, the velocity or the displacement, and derives the other
two from its own Newmark relations; its Neumann side receives the
d'Alembert reaction of the partner through the transpose of the
kinematic transfer, both transfers built from one cross-mass matrix; the
iteration stops on the interface jump and the force residual; and the
initial acceleration is computed on the coupled system. At the fixed
point the exchange solves the velocity-constrained coupled problem of
Gravouil and Combescure, of Prakash and Hjelmstad, and of Connors, Owen,
Kuberry, and Bochev, for which the rate invariant (the energy functional
evaluated on the velocity and the acceleration, the invariant of the
time-differentiated system) is conserved exactly for
linear elasticity, for any pair of Newmark schemes, for any mesh ratio,
and, with the coarse-step side as the Dirichlet side, for any integer
step ratio. The note gives the formulation and its proof, the algorithm,
a direct interface solve for two explicit subdomains, and the
verification on a cantilever beam and on nested cylinders: the matched
energy is constant to the Schwarz tolerance over $10^4$ steps in every
configuration measured, and to roundoff with the direct solve, while the
classical exchange inverts an element within $1$\,ms with implicit
subdomains. An appendix lists the input keys of every coupling and the
tests and examples that exercise them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:scope}

A solid body $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is decomposed into subdomains
$\Omega_k$ that are discretized and integrated in time independently, each
with its own mesh, its own time integrator, and its own time step, and
coupled by a Schwarz iteration: within each time window the subdomains are
solved in turn, exchanging interface data, until the data converge,
following Mota, Tezaur, and Phlipot~\cite{MotaTezaurPhlipot2022}. Norma
implements five transmission conditions, each a boundary condition type of
a subdomain input:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item the \emph{overlapping Dirichlet coupling} (\code{Schwarz overlap}):
  the subdomains overlap, and each receives on its boundary inside the
  partner the displacement, velocity, and acceleration interpolated from
  the partner's solution (Sec.~\ref{sec:overlap});
\item the \emph{classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange}
  (\code{Schwarz DN nonoverlap} without \code{matching}): the subdomains
  share an interface; one side receives the three kinematic fields of the
  other and the other receives the internal force of the first
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:classical});
\item the \emph{matching Dirichlet--Neumann exchange}
  (\code{Schwarz DN nonoverlap} with \code{matching}): one side matches the
  velocity or the displacement of the other and the other receives the
  d'Alembert reaction of the first, so that the fixed point of the
  iteration is the constrained coupled problem whose rate invariant is
  conserved (Sec.~\ref{sec:matching});
\item the \emph{Robin--Robin condition} (\code{Schwarz RR nonoverlap}):
  each side imposes $~t + \alpha ~W ~u = ~g$ on the shared interface, with
  the datum $~g$ formed from the partner's traction and displacement
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:rr});
\item the \emph{contact coupling} (\code{Schwarz contact}): two bodies
  whose surfaces may come into contact are coupled by a Dirichlet--Neumann
  exchange that is active while contact is detected
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}).
\end{enumerate}
All five share the controller of Sec.~\ref{sec:controller}, the
relaxation of Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}, and the transfer operators of
Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}. The note describes the code as of October 2026
and states, for each method, what it requires of the input and what it
does not do.

The requirements on a dynamic coupling, which only the matching exchange
satisfies in full, are:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Energy conservation.} At the converged state the coupling does
  no work on the system: the coupled problem conserves the quantity that
  the subdomain integrators conserve, to the tolerance of the Schwarz
  iteration.
\item \emph{Nonconforming meshes.} The meshes need not share nodes on the
  interface.
\item \emph{Heterogeneous integrators and time steps.} Each subdomain may use
  an implicit Newmark scheme or central difference, and the time steps may
  differ by an integer ratio.
\item \emph{Partitioned solution.} Each subdomain is advanced by its own
  solver; the coupling requires of a subdomain only operations that a
  structural dynamics code already provides (Sec.~\ref{sec:integration}).
\end{enumerate}
The classical exchange satisfies the last three but not the first: with
implicit subdomains on the beam of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification} it inverts
an element within $1$\,ms, and with explicit subdomains it conserves the
energy only to $2$ to $4\%$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}). The overlapping
coupling exchanges data on two different surfaces, so there is no shared
interface on which the works of the two sides can cancel. The Robin--Robin
condition is not absorbing and is used for quasi-static problems.

\section{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Semi-discrete equations.} After finite element discretization
in space, subdomain $k$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  ~M_k\, ~a_k + ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k(~u_k)
  = ~f^{\mathrm{ext}}_k + ~C_k^T ~\lambda_k ,
  \label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
with $~u_k$, $~v_k$, $~a_k$ the nodal displacement, velocity, and
acceleration, $~M_k$ the mass matrix (lumped for central difference),
$~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k$ the internal force, $~f^{\mathrm{ext}}_k$ the external
force, $~C_k$ the Boolean restriction of the nodal vector to the interface
nodes, and $~\lambda_k$ the interface force acting on subdomain $k$. For
linear elasticity $~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k = ~K_k ~u_k$. A quasi-static
subdomain omits the inertial term. The reference coordinates of the nodes
are $~X$, and the current coordinates are $~x = ~X + ~u$.

\paragraph{Time integrators.} A dynamic subdomain is advanced by the
Newmark scheme with parameters $(\beta, \gamma)$ and step $\Delta t$:
\begin{equation}
  ~u^{n+1} = ~u^{n} + \Delta t\, ~v^{n}
    + \Delta t^2 \bigl[(\tfrac12 - \beta)\, ~a^{n} + \beta\, ~a^{n+1}\bigr],
  \qquad
  ~v^{n+1} = ~v^{n} + \Delta t \bigl[(1 - \gamma)\, ~a^{n} + \gamma\, ~a^{n+1}\bigr].
  \label{eq:newmark}
\end{equation}
The trapezoidal rule ($\beta = 1/4$, $\gamma = 1/2$, implicit, denoted I)
and central difference ($\beta = 0$, $\gamma = 1/2$, explicit with a lumped
mass, denoted E) are the two schemes used in the verification. With the
predictors
$~u^{\mathrm{pred}} = ~u^n + \Delta t\, ~v^n + (\tfrac12 - \beta)\Delta t^2 ~a^n$
and $~v^{\mathrm{pred}} = ~v^n + (1-\gamma)\Delta t\, ~a^n$, the scheme gives
\begin{equation}
  ~a^{n+1} = \frac{~v^{n+1} - ~v^{\mathrm{pred}}}{\gamma\, \Delta t},
  \qquad
  ~u^{n+1} = ~u^{\mathrm{pred}} + \beta\, \Delta t^2\, ~a^{n+1},
  \label{eq:newmark-from-v}
\end{equation}
so a prescribed end-of-step velocity determines the acceleration and the
displacement of the step, and, for $\beta > 0$,
\begin{equation}
  ~a^{n+1} = \frac{~u^{n+1} - ~u^{\mathrm{pred}}}{\beta\, \Delta t^2},
  \qquad
  ~v^{n+1} = ~v^{\mathrm{pred}} + \gamma\, \Delta t\, ~a^{n+1},
  \label{eq:newmark-from-u}
\end{equation}
so a prescribed end-of-step displacement determines the other two. An
implicit subdomain solves its step by Newton's method on the displacement
with the Hessian minimizer of Norma; an explicit subdomain divides the
residual by the lumped mass. The Newmark predictor and corrector update
only the free degrees of freedom of a subdomain, so a value prescribed on a
degree of freedom before the step persists through it.

\paragraph{Prescribed rows.} A boundary condition prescribes a value on a
set of degrees of freedom and marks them not free. Before every subdomain
solve the boundary force vector is cleared, every degree of freedom is
marked free, and the boundary conditions are applied in order, those whose
type name ends in \code{Dirichlet} last, so that a prescribed displacement
overrides a coupling datum on a shared node. The coupling conditions of
this note either prescribe interface degrees of freedom (Dirichlet type) or
add to the boundary force vector (Neumann type). The Newmark initial
acceleration solves $~M_{ff}\, ~a_f = (~f^{\mathrm{ext}} - ~f^{\mathrm{int}})_f -
~M_{fc}\, ~a_c$ for the free rows $f$ given the prescribed rows $c$. The
rows held by a Schwarz coupling carry the partner's projected
acceleration, which is zero until the partner has computed its own. On a
start from initial conditions those rows are therefore excluded from the
set $c$ of the correction; they are included only in the refinement passes of
Sec.~\ref{sec:initialization} and in the coupled initial acceleration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:initial}, once every subdomain has an acceleration.

\paragraph{Roles.} In a Dirichlet--Neumann exchange the \emph{Dirichlet
side} $D$ receives a kinematic datum on its interface nodes and the
\emph{Neumann side} $N$ receives a force. A pair of integrators is written
with the integrator of one named subdomain first (II, IE, EI, EE).

\paragraph{Interface operators.} Let $\varphi^D_m$ and $\varphi^N_n$ be the
interface traces of the nodal basis functions of the two sides of a shared
interface $\Gamma$. The cross-mass matrix and the boundary mass matrices are
\begin{equation}
  B_{mn} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^D_m\, \varphi^N_n \,\dd S, \qquad
  (~W_D)_{ml} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^D_m\, \varphi^D_l \,\dd S, \qquad
  (~W_N)_{nl} = \int_\Gamma \varphi^N_n\, \varphi^N_l \,\dd S,
  \label{eq:cross-mass}
\end{equation}
assembled once on the reference configuration, per Cartesian component.
The kinematic transfer to the Dirichlet side and its transpose are
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_D = ~W_D^{-1} ~B, \qquad \Pi_D^T = ~B^T ~W_D^{-1} .
  \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
$\Pi_D$ maps the interface values of side $N$ to the $L^2$ projection of
their trace onto the trace space of side $D$; it reproduces constants when
$~B\,\mathbf{1} = ~W_D \mathbf{1}$, that is, when the quadrature of $~B$ is
exact on products of the two trace spaces. Section~\ref{sec:transfer}
gives the assembly of these matrices and the variants that the other
couplings use.

\paragraph{Stops and windows.} The Schwarz controller advances in
\emph{stops} of length $\Delta t_c$. Within a stop each subdomain advances
to the stop end in its own steps; the \emph{Schwarz iteration} repeats a
solve of each subdomain with interface data exchanged between the solves
until the data converge, and the stop is then committed. A stop that spans
$m$ steps of a subdomain is a \emph{window} of $m$ substeps for that
subdomain.

\paragraph{Energies.} The energy is
\begin{equation}
  E = \sum_k \Bigl(\tfrac12\, ~v_k^T ~M_k\, ~v_k + \mathrm{SE}(~u_k)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{SE}$ the strain energy. For central difference the kinetic
term is evaluated in the staggered form
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\, (~v^{n-1/2})^T ~M\, ~v^{n+1/2}
  = \tfrac12\, (~v^n)^T ~M\, ~v^n - \tfrac{\Delta t^2}{8}\, (~a^n)^T ~M\, ~a^n ,
  \label{eq:staggered}
\end{equation}
the product of the two half-step velocities that bracket $t_n$, written in
terms of the integer-step velocity $~v^n = \tfrac12(~v^{n-1/2} + ~v^{n+1/2})$
and the acceleration, since $~v^{n \pm 1/2} = ~v^n \pm \tfrac{\Delta t}{2} ~a^n$.
With the strain energy at $~u^n$ it is the quadratic
$\tfrac12 (~v^{n-1/2})^T ~M ~v^{n+1/2} + \tfrac12 (~u^n)^T ~K ~u^n$ that
central difference conserves exactly on a linear problem, evaluated at the
integer step; it is not the kinetic energy at a half step, which is
$\tfrac12 (~v^{n+1/2})^T ~M ~v^{n+1/2} = \tfrac12 (~v^n)^T ~M ~v^n
+ \tfrac{\Delta t}{2} (~v^n)^T ~M ~a^n + \tfrac{\Delta t^2}{8} (~a^n)^T ~M ~a^n$
and is conserved only together with the product strain energy
$\tfrac12 (~u^n)^T ~K ~u^{n+1}$. The form \eqref{eq:staggered} pairs with
$\mathrm{SE}(~u^n)$ and therefore extends to nonlinear materials. The
consistent-mass kinetic energy $\tfrac12 (~v^n)^T ~M ~v^n$ at integer steps
misrepresents the short-wavelength content of a sharp front by several
percent. For linear elasticity the rate invariant is
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{E} = \sum_k \Bigl(\tfrac12\, ~a_k^T ~S_k\, ~a_k
             + \tfrac12\, ~v_k^T ~K_k\, ~v_k\Bigr),
  \qquad
  ~S_k = ~M_k + \Bigl(\beta_k - \frac{\gamma_k}{2}\Bigr)\Delta t_k^2\, ~K_k ,
  \label{eq:rate-invariant}
\end{equation}
the energy functional evaluated on the velocity and the acceleration. The
time derivative of a solution of $~M \ddot{~u} + ~K ~u = ~f$ solves
$~M \dot{~a} + ~K ~v = \dot{~f}$, so this quadratic is the invariant of the
time-differentiated system; it is not an energy, and its units are those of
energy divided by time squared. $~S_k$ is $~M_k$ for the trapezoidal rule and
$~M_k - \tfrac14 \Delta t_k^2 ~K_k$ for central difference, positive
definite exactly below the stability limit of central difference. Relative
to $E$, $\tilde{E}$ weights each vibration mode by the square of its
frequency.

\section{The controller and the Schwarz iteration}
\label{sec:controller}

A multidomain input (\code{type: multi}) lists the subdomain inputs in
\code{domains} and sets the time window, the controller step, and the
parameters of the iteration (Appendix~\ref{app:keys}). The controller
supplies the time window to every subdomain and clamps each subdomain's
step to the controller step.

\subsection{Stops and substeps}
\label{sec:stops}

The controller time after stop $s$ is $t_s = t_0 + s\,\Delta t_c$; the
number of stops is $\mathrm{round}((t_f - t_0)/\Delta t_c)$, and the span
$t_f - t_0$ must be a multiple of $\Delta t_c$. The step of subdomain $k$ is
$\Delta t_k = \min(\Delta t_k^{\mathrm{in}}, \Delta t_c)$, with
$\Delta t_k^{\mathrm{in}}$ its own input step (default $\Delta t_c$); for
central difference it is further limited to the stable step of the mesh.
Within a stop the subdomain takes substeps of $\Delta t_k$ from $t_{s-1}$,
the last substep taking a remainder of up to $1.5\,\Delta t_k$, until it
reaches $t_s$ to a relative tolerance of $10^{-6}$. A stop of $m_k$
substeps is a window of $m_k$ for subdomain $k$. No check enforces that
$\Delta t_c$ is a multiple of $\Delta t_k$; the substep count is whatever
this rule produces.

\subsection{One Schwarz iteration}
\label{sec:iteration}

At the start of a stop the controller saves the state of every subdomain
at $t_{s-1}$ (displacement, velocity, acceleration, internal force, and the
material state), clears the partner histories and the relaxation state
(Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}), swaps the roles of the pairs that request it
(Sec.~\ref{sec:classical}), and, if the interface predictor is enabled,
forms the predictor of Sec.~\ref{sec:predictor}. Iteration $i$ of the stop
then proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Every subdomain is reset to $t_{s-1}$.
\item The subdomains are advanced one after another, in the order of
  \code{domains}. Subdomain $k$ first clears its own history and records
  the anchor $(t_{s-1}, ~u_k, ~v_k, ~a_k, ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k)$; then for
  each substep it applies its boundary conditions at the substep end time,
  solves the substep, and records $(t, ~u_k, ~v_k, ~a_k, ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k)$.
\item A coupling condition of subdomain $k$ applied at time $t$ reads the
  partner $j$'s displacement, velocity, acceleration, and internal force by
  piecewise-linear interpolation in time of the partner's history,
  \begin{equation}
    ~x_j(t) = \frac{t_{l+1} - t}{t_{l+1} - t_l}\, ~x_j^{\,l}
            + \frac{t - t_l}{t_{l+1} - t_l}\, ~x_j^{\,l+1},
    \qquad t_l \le t \le t_{l+1},
    \label{eq:interp}
  \end{equation}
  with the end values returned outside the recorded range. If the partner's
  history is empty, the condition uses the predictor when one is enabled,
  the partner's state at $t_{s-1}$ otherwise.
\item After the pass over all subdomains the convergence tests of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping} are evaluated. If the stop has not converged, the
  end-of-iteration state is saved as the comparison iterate for the next
  test, and step 1 is repeated.
\end{enumerate}
The iteration is multiplicative: subdomain $k$ reads from a partner listed
before it the history of the current iteration, and from a partner listed
after it the history of the previous iteration; at $i = 0$ the latter is
empty, so the first-listed subdomain reads its partner's state at
$t_{s-1}$, constant in time, or the predictor. Every iteration restarts
the subdomain solves from the state at $t_{s-1}$, not from the previous
iterate.

\subsection{Stopping criteria}
\label{sec:stopping}

\paragraph{Update criterion.} With $\Delta t_c$ the controller step and
$~w_k = ~u_k + \Delta t_c\, ~v_k$ over all degrees of freedom of subdomain
$k$ (prescribed ones included), the update between iterations $i-1$ and
$i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta U = \Bigl( \sum_k \norm{~w_k^{\,i} - ~w_k^{\,i-1}}^2 \Bigr)^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \Delta u = \Delta U \Big/ \Bigl( \sum_k \norm{~w_k^{\,i}}^2 \Bigr)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
and the stop has converged when $\Delta U \le$ \code{absolute tolerance}
or $\Delta u \le$ \code{relative tolerance}. At $i = 0$ the comparison
iterate is the state at $t_{s-1}$, so $\Delta U$ is the change over the
whole step and the relative test does not apply. This criterion stops the
overlapping, classical Dirichlet--Neumann, Robin--Robin, and contact
couplings.

\paragraph{Matching criterion.} A matching pair converges when the
one-sided jump of its matched quantity and its force residual are at or
below \code{matching relative tolerance}, as defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping}. When every Schwarz coupling of the
simulation is a matching pair, this criterion alone decides, also at
$i = 0$, and the controller's \code{relative tolerance} and
\code{absolute tolerance} are not used (given, they are ignored with a
warning). When the simulation has couplings
of both kinds, both criteria must hold.

\paragraph{Iteration count.} A stop ends at iteration $i \ge 1$ when the
criterion holds and $i \ge$ \code{minimum iterations}, or when
$i >$ \code{maximum iterations}, or when $\Delta U = 0$. At $i = 0$ it
ends without further iterations when $\Delta U \le$
\code{absolute tolerance} (or, for a simulation of matching pairs only,
when the matching criterion holds) and every matching pair has converged.
A stop that reaches \code{maximum iterations} without converging aborts
the run (\code{unconverged step action: abort}, the default) with the
measures that apply, or continues with the last iterate
(\code{warn}).

\paragraph{Iterations not accepted.} An iteration at which a relaxation factor of magnitude
at most $10^{-12}$ was applied is not accepted as converged, because a
factor of zero leaves the interface datum unchanged and the subdomains then
return the solution they already had (Sec.~\ref{sec:aitken}). Under
\code{tight final solve} (Sec.~\ref{sec:inexact-newton}) convergence is
accepted only at an iteration whose Newton tolerance was the floor.

\subsection{Windowed stops and relaxation slots}
\label{sec:slots}

The relaxation state of a coupling pair (Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}) is kept
per pair and per \emph{slot}, a substep end time at which the relaxed side
applies its condition; a stop that spans several substeps of the relaxed
side has several slots. The state is cleared at every stop. A state shared
across substeps would combine iterates of different times, which acts as
a filter on the exchanged datum and moves the converged solution. Aitken and
Anderson acceleration apply to stops of one slot; a stop of several slots
uses the fixed factor \code{relaxation parameter}. Windowed and
single-substep runs of the same problem agree to the Schwarz truncation
error, and the total number of substep solves is proportional to the
iterations per stop, so a window does not reduce the number of subdomain
solves.

\subsection{Control of the Newton tolerance of the subdomain solves}
\label{sec:inexact-newton}

The optional block \code{inexact Newton} sets the relative Newton tolerance
of every implicit subdomain before the solves of each Schwarz iteration:
the ceiling \code{maximum tolerance} (default $10^{-2}$) at $i = 0$, and
\begin{equation}
  \tau_i = \mathrm{clamp}\bigl(\eta\, r_{i-1},\ \tau_{\min},\ \tau_{\max}\bigr),
  \qquad i \ge 1,
  \label{eq:inexact}
\end{equation}
with $\eta$ the \code{tolerance ratio} (default $0.01$), $\tau_{\min}$ the
\code{minimum tolerance} (default $10^{-12}$), and $r_{i-1}$ the relative
Schwarz residual of the previous iteration: the matching measure of
Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping} when every coupling is a matching pair,
$\Delta u$ of \eqref{eq:update} when there is none, and the larger of the
two otherwise. A Newton solve stops when its residual norm is at or below
the solver's absolute tolerance or its ratio to the initial residual norm
is at or below $\tau_i$; the absolute tolerance of the subdomain
\code{solver} block still applies. Because every Schwarz iteration restarts
from the state at $t_{s-1}$, a constant Newton tolerance larger than the
Schwarz tolerance leaves an error of the size of the Newton error that the
displacement criterion does not detect; the ratio $\eta < 1$ keeps the
Newton error of the accepted iterate below the Schwarz update by the factor
$\eta$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:inexact}). With \code{tight final solve: true} a
convergence reached at $\tau_i > \tau_{\min}$ is rejected and the iteration
continues with $\tau_{\min}$ for the rest of the stop. The control acts on
Hessian minimizer solvers only; explicit subdomains are not affected.

\subsection{Initialization}
\label{sec:initialization}

Before the first stop the controller builds the transfer operators of
every coupling (Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}) on the reference configuration,
applies the initial conditions, records a history snapshot of every
subdomain, applies the boundary conditions, and computes the initial
acceleration of every dynamic subdomain with the coupling rows excluded
from the correction of Sec.~\ref{sec:setting}. If the run is a restart, or
a Robin--Robin interface has nonzero initial displacement or velocity, two
refinement passes follow, each applying the boundary conditions from the
updated histories and recomputing the initial accelerations with the
coupling rows included in the correction of Sec.~\ref{sec:setting}.
Matching pairs then compute the coupled initial acceleration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:initial}, and contact pairs evaluate their detection
(Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}).

\subsection{Output}
\label{sec:output}

Exodus and CSV output are written at the controller stops that are
multiples of \code{Exodus output interval} (default $\Delta t_c$) and
\code{CSV output interval} (default off). With
\code{energy output: true} the controller writes
\code{<name>-energy.csv} at every stop, with the columns \code{time},
\code{stored\_energy}, \code{kinetic\_energy}, \code{total\_energy}
($E$ of \eqref{eq:energy}, blended over overlaps as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:blended}), \code{rate\_invariant\_total} and one
\code{rate\_invariant\_<domain>} per subdomain ($\tilde{E}$ of
\eqref{eq:rate-invariant}, NaN unless every material is linear elastic and
every integrator is central difference or Newmark without HHT-$\alpha$),
\code{staggered\_kinetic\_interface} and
\code{staggered\_kinetic\_interior} (the central difference kinetic term
split between interface and interior nodes), and, for every
Dirichlet--Neumann pair, the relative one-sided jumps
\code{displacement\_jump\_<D>\_<N>} and \code{velocity\_jump\_<D>\_<N>}
of Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping} with their root mean square values
(\code{\_rms}) and, for matching pairs, the impulse residual
\eqref{eq:impulse} per component and its relative value, followed by
\code{stored\_energy\_<domain>}, \code{kinetic\_energy\_<domain>},
and \code{total\_energy\_<domain>} for every subdomain, its contribution
to $E$, which the totals sum. The kinetic energy of a central difference
subdomain in this file is the staggered form \eqref{eq:staggered}, not the
$\tfrac12 (~v^n)^T ~M ~v^n$ that the subdomain writes in its own output. The
rate invariant and staggered columns are not blended over overlaps.

\subsection{Swaps and restart}
\label{sec:swaps}

A \code{swaps} list replaces a subdomain by another input between stops
when a criterion holds: a time, a stress recovery estimate, an elastic to
plastic transition, or the overlap $L^2$ error of Sec.~\ref{sec:overlap-l2}
against a tolerance in a direction (\code{refine} or \code{coarsen}). The
replacement receives the state of the subdomain it replaces, by copy on
the same mesh or by $L^2$ transfer between meshes; the coupling conditions
and transfer operators are rebuilt after a swap. A restart of a multidomain
run resumes every subdomain from the same checkpoint time; it is allowed
with the overlapping coupling, warned against with the nonoverlapping
couplings, and rejected with contact.
\section{Relaxation and acceleration of the iteration}
\label{sec:relaxation}

The datum that a coupling transmits is relaxed on one side of each pair:
the Dirichlet side of a Dirichlet--Neumann or contact pair, and the
later-listed side of a Robin--Robin pair. The overlapping coupling is not
relaxed. Let $~g_i$ be the datum computed from the partner at iteration
$i$ and $~\lambda_{i-1}$ the datum applied at the previous iteration of
the same slot; a new slot has no previous datum and uses $~g_i$
unrelaxed (the Robin--Robin datum starts from zero, Sec.~\ref{sec:rr}).
The input key \code{relaxation} selects one of four schemes, with
\code{relaxation parameter} $\theta_0$ (default $1$; $0.5$ under
Anderson acceleration).

\paragraph{Fixed.} $~\lambda_i = \theta_0\, ~g_i + (1 - \theta_0)\, ~\lambda_{i-1}$.

\subsection{Aitken relaxation}
\label{sec:aitken}

With the residual $~r_i = ~g_i - ~\lambda_{i-1}$ and
$~\delta_i = ~r_i - ~r_{i-1}$, the \emph{recursive} form
(\code{aitken recursive}, Irons and Tuck) applies
$~\lambda_i = \theta_i ~g_i + (1 - \theta_i) ~\lambda_{i-1}$ with
\begin{equation}
  \theta_i = -\,\theta_{i-1}\, \frac{~r_{i-1}^T ~\delta_i}{\norm{~\delta_i}^2},
  \label{eq:aitken-recursive}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{secant} form (\code{aitken secant}) applies
\begin{equation}
  \theta_i = -\,\frac{~d_i^T ~\delta_i}{\norm{~\delta_i}^2}, \qquad
  ~d_i = ~\lambda_{i-1} - ~\lambda_{i-2}.
  \label{eq:aitken-secant}
\end{equation}
Both use $\theta_0$ for the first \code{aitken N0 parameter} iterations
(default $1$) and whenever the required previous residuals do not exist.
The factor is not clamped. The update is skipped, and the previous factor
(recursive) or $\theta_0$ (secant) kept, when
$\norm{~\delta_i}^2 \le (10^{-14})^2 \norm{~\lambda_{i-1}}^2$, so that a
pair whose datum has stopped moving does not produce a factor of zero. A
factor of magnitude at most $10^{-12}$ is recorded, and the iteration at
which it was applied is not accepted as converged
(Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping}): a zero factor leaves the interface datum at
its previous value, every subdomain then re-solves against the data it
already had, and the update that follows measures the
effect of an unchanged datum, not the interface residual. For a Dirichlet--Neumann pair the first secant factor
appears at iteration $2$: at iteration $0$ the new slot has $~r = 0$,
and at iteration $1$ the difference $~d$ is zero.

The vector on which the factor is formed depends on the coupling. For the
classical Dirichlet--Neumann and contact exchanges it is the partner's
whole displacement field; for the matching exchange it is the projected
interface trace $\Pi_D ~q_N$ of the matched quantity, because the interior
values do not enter the exchange, follow a map of zero gain, and dominate
the residual, which drives the factor toward $1$ where the interface map,
of gain near $-1$, does not contract. For the Robin--Robin condition it is
the Robin datum itself.

\subsection{Anderson acceleration}
\label{sec:anderson}

Anderson acceleration~\cite{WalkerNi2011} (\code{anderson}), in the
interface quasi-Newton form of Degroote, Bathe, and
Vierendeels~\cite{DegrooteBatheVierendeels2009}, combines the last $m$
iterates (\code{anderson depth}, default $10$). With $~x_i$ the datum
applied at iteration $i$, $~f_i = ~g_i - ~x_i$ its residual, $~t_i$ the
trace of the residual on which the coefficients are formed, and
$\Delta ~X$, $\Delta ~F$, $\Delta ~T$ the matrices of differences of the
last $m + 1$ iterates,
\begin{equation}
  ~\gamma = \arg\min \norm{~t_i - \Delta ~T\, ~\gamma}, \qquad
  ~x_{i+1} = ~x_i + \beta\, ~f_i - (\Delta ~X + \beta\, \Delta ~F)\, ~\gamma ,
  \label{eq:anderson}
\end{equation}
with the mixing parameter $\beta$ (\code{relaxation parameter}, default
$0.5$). The least-squares problem is solved by QR, with the oldest column
dropped while the condition number of the triangular factor exceeds
$10^{10}$. The first iteration of a slot has no history and is the fixed
relaxation $~x_1 = ~x_0 + \beta ~f_0$; it is not entered in the history.
With $\beta = 1$ and a depth at least the iteration count the iterate
equals the fixed-point map applied to the GMRES iterate of the same index
on a linear interface map (Walker and Ni, Theorem~2.2), which test 136
checks to $10^{-8}$ on a probed interface map. The trace $~t_i$ is the
projected interface residual $\Pi_D(~g_i - ~x_i)$ of the displacement for
the classical and contact exchanges (one set of coefficients then updates
the displacement, velocity, and acceleration, each with its own history),
of the matched quantity for the matching exchange, and the residual of the
Robin datum for the Robin--Robin condition. One least-squares problem is
solved for all relaxed interfaces of the simulation: their traces are
stacked into one vector $~t_i$, the matrices $\Delta ~T$ are stacked
accordingly, and the coefficients $~\gamma$ update every interface, as in
Sambataro and Tezaur~\cite{SambataroTezaurAnderson}. The interfaces are grouped by the relaxed quantity
(interface displacement, interface velocity, or Robin datum), with one
problem per group. Coefficients computed for each interface separately fit
one secant model per interface, although the residual of an interface
depends on the data of the others. On a disk in a matrix whose interface
is coupled as four side sets (test 139, relaxation parameter $0.2$,
depth $20$) they inverted an element, whereas the stacked problem
converges in $23$ iterations and the interface as one side set in $21$
(issue \#233). The stacked step is computed when the first relaxed
interface of an iteration applies its datum, from the partner data at that
moment; an interface whose partner is solved after that moment and before
its own subdomain receives the partner data of the previous iteration. The
histories are cleared at every stop and after a stall
(Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping}).
On the beam of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification} ($1$\,ms, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$)
$\beta = 0.5$ needs $7.0$, $23.1$, and $7.9$ iterations per window on the
implicit pair, on the mixed pair with the explicit side as the Dirichlet
side, and on the explicit pair at mesh ratio $1{:}0.5$ with $4{:}1$ steps,
against $8.9$, $28.7$, and $8.8$ at $\beta = 1$, with the same energies;
for the matching exchange it is the recommended scheme.

\section{Interface transfer operators}
\label{sec:transfer}

\subsection{Point location in a partner mesh}
\label{sec:location}

A node or quadrature point of one subdomain is located in the volume mesh
of a partner by a search over the elements of a named block
(\code{source block}) with a bounding-box grid built on the reference
configuration; a candidate element is accepted when the point lies within
its bounding box enlarged by $10\%$ and its parametric coordinates
$~\xi$, found by Newton's method on $\sum_a N_a(~\xi) ~X_a = ~X$ to
$10^{-8}$, satisfy the parametric inside test enlarged by the
\code{search tolerance} (default $10^{-6}$, dimensionless): for a
hexahedron $|\xi_c| \le 1 + \mathrm{tol}$ in every direction, for a
tetrahedron $\sum_c \xi_c \le 1 + \mathrm{tol}$. The first element that
passes is used, and a point found in no element aborts the run. The shape
function values $N_a(~\xi)$ are stored once, on the reference
configuration, and reused at every step.

A point of a facet of one side of a shared interface is located on the
partner's side set by a closest-point projection onto the partner facets,
with a bounding-box prefilter by the nearest partner node; the facet whose
parametric overshoot is at most $10^{-6}$ is preferred, with the distance
deciding between equal overshoots, and the facet of the nearest node is
used when no facet satisfies the overshoot bound.
The search uses the partner's current coordinates, which are the reference
coordinates when the operators are built at initialization.

\subsection{Boundary mass and cross-mass matrices}
\label{sec:mass-matrices}

The boundary mass matrix $~W$ of a side set is the consistent facet mass
matrix \eqref{eq:cross-mass} on the reference coordinates, with the
default facet quadrature of each facet type (three points for a linear
triangle, four for a bilinear quadrilateral, seven for a quadratic
triangle). The cross-mass matrix of a destination side $d$ and a source
side $s$ of a shared interface,
\begin{equation}
  L^{d s}_{mn} = \sum_{\text{facets of } d}\ \sum_{q}
    \varphi^d_m(~x_q)\, \varphi^s_n(~\xi_s(~x_q))\, J_q\, w_q ,
  \label{eq:cross-dst}
\end{equation}
is integrated over the facets of the destination. Since October 18, 2026,
the quadrature is segment based: each destination facet is split into its
intersections with the source facets, projected onto the plane of the
destination facet, and each piece is integrated with a seven-point
triangle rule of degree five. The facets may be three-node triangles,
four-node quadrilaterals, or straight-sided six-node triangles (the facets
of ten-node tetrahedra) on either side, in any combination: the shape
functions of a triangle are polynomials of degree one or two in the plane
for any triangle shape, those of a parallelogram quadrilateral of degree
two, so their products are of degree at most four on such facets. On
interfaces of planar triangles and parallelograms $~L$ is therefore exact
for any mesh ratio, the two integration directions give the same matrix,
and $~P_d$ below reproduces linear fields (checked to roundoff on the beam
at ratio $1{:}0.75$ with the free part remeshed in linear and in quadratic
tetrahedra against the hexahedral clamped part, October 18, 2026). A
six-node facet whose midside nodes depart from the edge midpoints by more
than $10^{-8}$ of its longest edge is curved and takes the fallback rule
below. The boundary
mass matrix $~W_d$ that enters the projectors is integrated facet by facet
with the same quadrature as $~L$, so that the row sums of the two agree
exactly on every facet, also where the rule, which integrates the shape
functions in physical coordinates, is inexact (on the paved cap facets of
the nested cylinders it differs from the facet's Gauss rule by up to
$10^{-2}$; on the beam the two agree to roundoff); $~P_d$ therefore
reproduces constants and the transposed transfer conserves the total force
exactly on every interface. A facet that the projected
source facets do not cover to a relative $10^{-8}$ of its area (a gap, a
partial overlap, or a curved interface, on which the projected neighbors
overlap or leave gaps of the order of the sagitta) is integrated with its
own Gauss rule, each quadrature point located on the source by the
projection of Sec.~\ref{sec:location}, and the setup log reports the
count of such facets. That rule, which was the only one before, carries
the quadrature error of the kinks of the source basis inside the facet.
Integrated on the facets of the Dirichlet side it reproduces constants and
linear fields, but the entries of $~B$ are in error by $9\times 10^{-3}$
to $1.1\times 10^{-2}$ on the beam at ratio $1{:}0.75$, and the
transposed force transfer distributes a uniform traction over the Neumann
nodes with a strain error of $4\times 10^{-3}$ that rises to
$7\times 10^{-3}$ when both meshes are refined once (static patch test of
the matching exchange, October 18, 2026); with the segmented rule the same
tests are reproduced to roundoff. The matrix integrated over the source facets is $(L^{s d})^T$.
Two projections are built from these matrices:
\begin{align}
  \text{Dirichlet projector}\quad & ~P_d = ~W_d^{-1} ~L, \label{eq:dirichlet-projector} \\
  \text{Neumann projector}\quad & ~N_d = ~L\, ~W_s^{-1}, \label{eq:neumann-projector}
\end{align}
$~P_d$ mapping nodal values of the source to the $L^2$ projection of their
trace onto the destination, and $~N_d$ mapping nodal forces of the source
to nodal forces of the destination. For $~P_d$ the code prefers the
source-integrated $~L$ and uses the destination-integrated one instead
when $\max|~P_d \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}| > 10^{-10}$; for $~N_d$ it prefers
the destination-integrated $~L$ and uses the other instead when the
relative error of the column sums of $~L$ against $~W_s \mathbf{1}$ exceeds
$10^{-10}$. The classical Dirichlet--Neumann, Robin--Robin, and contact
couplings build $~P_d$ and $~N_d$ independently on each side by these
rules. The matching exchange instead builds both sides' transfers from one
cross-mass matrix $~B$, with $\Pi_D = ~W_D^{-1} ~B$ and the force transfer
$\Pi_D^T$. $~B$ is integrated over the facets of the Neumann side when
$\Pi_D$ then reproduces constants to $10^{-12}$, which happens when those
facets each lie within a facet of the Dirichlet side; that quadrature
evaluates $~B$ exactly. On nested facets with the coarser mesh as the
Dirichlet side, the quadrature of the Dirichlet side does not, and the
iteration of the beam at $1{:}0.5$ with $4{:}1$ steps (trapezoidal pair, fixed
factor $0.5$) needs $61.9$ Schwarz iterations per window against $44.5$.
Otherwise $~B$ is integrated over the facets of the Dirichlet side with the
same quadrature as $~W_D$. On that side the row sums of $~B$ equal
those of $~W_D$ exactly, because the trace basis of the Neumann side sums to
one at every quadrature point, so $\Pi_D$ reproduces constants to roundoff
and the total force transferred to the Neumann side equals the total
reaction of the Dirichlet side. The partition-of-unity error
$\max|\Pi_D \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}|$ is logged, with that of the unused
projection $~W_N^{-1} ~B^T$ for information. Before October 16, 2026, $~B$
was integrated over the facets of the side with more interface nodes, which
is the Dirichlet side in every run of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification}; with the
coarser mesh as the Dirichlet side the two rules differ on facets that are
not nested, and the earlier rule gave $\Pi_D$ a partition-of-unity error
of $1.2\times 10^{-2}$ on the beam at ratio $1{:}0.75$ and $0.72$ on the
cylinders. The energies are conserved to the same digits under either
rule, since the identities of Sec.~\ref{sec:identity} hold for any $~B$;
the error is in the solution: a rigid translation of the beam at $10$\,m/s
for $1$\,ms then deviates by up to $3.6\%$ in the nodal velocities with
$4.5\%$ of the kinetic energy turned into strain energy, and of the
cylinders at $5$\,m/s for $0.2$\,ms by up to $67\%$ with $21\%$ of the
kinetic energy turned into strain energy, against $5\times 10^{-11}$ and
$2\times 10^{-10}$ with $~B$ on the Dirichlet side (run data in
\code{\textasciitilde/Projects/Norma-Schwarz-Coupling/integration-side-2026-10}). Only $\Pi_D$ and its transpose enter the matching exchange; the
projection onto the other side, $~W_N^{-1} ~B^T$, is never applied. It
reproduces constants only to the quadrature error of $~B$: with the finer
mesh as the Dirichlet side, $\max|~W_N^{-1} ~B^T \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}|$
is $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ on the beam at mesh ratio $1{:}0.75$ and $0.72$ on
the cylinders of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification}; with the coarser mesh as the
Dirichlet side it is $0.28$ and $1.34$, and $1.14$ on the beam at mesh
ratio $1{:}0.5$, where the facets are nested but the quadrature of the
coarser side does not evaluate $~B$ exactly. The error of $\Pi_D$ is near
$10^{-15}$ in every case.

\paragraph{Displacement, not position.} A Dirichlet--Neumann transfer
projects the partner's displacement, $~P_d ~u_s$. Projecting the current
position $~P_d(~X_s + ~u_s)$ and subtracting the destination's reference
position $~X_d$ is not equivalent on a curved interface, because
$~P_d ~X_s \ne ~X_d$ by the sagitta of the facets between the two meshes;
that difference is a spurious displacement of the size of the chord
error. The overlapping and contact couplings transfer the position, as
their formulation requires (Secs.~\ref{sec:overlap} and
\ref{sec:contact}).

\section{The overlapping Dirichlet coupling}
\label{sec:overlap}

Two subdomains overlap; each has a side set $\Gamma_k$ of its own boundary
that lies inside the partner. The coupling is a Dirichlet condition on
$\Gamma_k$ with the datum interpolated from the partner's solution; both
sides are Dirichlet, there is no force transfer, and no role is assigned.

\paragraph{Strong form (the default).} For each node $i$ of $\Gamma_k$,
the element of the partner's \code{source block} that contains its
reference position $~X_i$ and the shape function values $N_a$ at that point
are found once (Sec.~\ref{sec:location}). At every application the
condition prescribes, with the partner fields interpolated in time by
\eqref{eq:interp},
\begin{equation}
  ~u_i = \sum_a N_a\, (~X^{\,j}_a + ~u^{\,j}_a) - ~X_i, \qquad
  ~v_i = \sum_a N_a\, ~v^{\,j}_a, \qquad
  ~a_i = \sum_a N_a\, ~a^{\,j}_a ,
  \label{eq:overlap-strong}
\end{equation}
and marks the three components of node $i$ not free. The displacement is
the interpolated current position minus the node's own reference position,
which equals the interpolated displacement up to the residual of the
parametric inversion.

\paragraph{Weak form (\code{weak: true}).} The datum is the $L^2$
projection of the partner's field onto the trace space of $\Gamma_k$:
$~P = ~W_k^{-1} ~L$, with $~W_k$ the boundary mass matrix of $\Gamma_k$ and
$~L$ the rectangular matrix of \eqref{eq:cross-dst} whose quadrature points
on the facets of $\Gamma_k$ are located in the partner's volume elements,
so that $~L$ has one column per node of the partner. The condition
prescribes $~u|_{\Gamma_k} = ~P(~X^{\,j} + ~u^{\,j}) - ~X|_{\Gamma_k}$,
$~v|_{\Gamma_k} = ~P ~v^{\,j}$, $~a|_{\Gamma_k} = ~P ~a^{\,j}$.

\paragraph{Properties.} The three fields are prescribed in both the
dynamic and the quasi-static case; a quasi-static subdomain uses only the
displacement, and a Newmark subdomain with a consistent mass receives the
prescribed acceleration in its free rows through the coupling term of the
mass matrix. The element search and the shape function values are fixed
at construction on the reference configuration and are not updated for
finite deformation. The datum is not relaxed: the relaxation keys have no
effect on this coupling, and the interface predictor does not apply to it.
The iteration stops by the update criterion of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping}.

\subsection{Overlap \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} error}
\label{sec:overlap-l2}

With \code{compute overlap L2 relative error: disp|velo|acce} the
coupling records, for the set $\mathcal{O}$ of nodes of subdomain $k$
that lie in the partner's \code{source block} (the nodes of elements whose
centroid, or any node, is found there, restricted to those found
themselves), the relative nodal error of the selected field $~\phi$,
\begin{equation}
  e_k = \Bigl( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}} \bigl\lVert ~\phi_i
        - \sum_a N_a(~\xi_i)\, ~\phi^{\,j}_a \bigr\rVert^2
        \Big/ \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}} \norm{~\phi_i}^2 \Bigr)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:overlap-l2}
\end{equation}
an unweighted nodal norm of the current fields, reported at the end of
every stop that took at least one Schwarz iteration and written to
\code{overlap-l2-<field>-rel-errors-<stop>.csv} at the CSV output times.
It is also the criterion of the overlap $L^2$ swap of
Sec.~\ref{sec:swaps}.

\subsection{Blended energy over the overlap}
\label{sec:blended}

The overlap region is meshed twice, so the sum of the subdomain energies
counts it twice. The energy output weights the energy density at
each quadrature point $~x$ of subdomain $k$ by the Arlequin partition of
unity
\begin{equation}
  w_k(~x) = \frac{d_k(~x)}{d_k(~x) + d_j(~x)}
  \label{eq:arlequin}
\end{equation}
when $~x$ lies in the partner $j$, and $w_k = 1$ otherwise, with $d_k$ the
distance from $~x$ to the overlap side set $\Gamma_k$ of subdomain $k$ and
$d_j$ the distance to $\Gamma_j$, both on the reference configuration and
each the minimum over the facets of the side set of the distance to the
closest point of the facet. Membership is decided by locating $~x$ in the
partner mesh (all blocks) with the \code{search tolerance}, so the meshes
need not conform. For two subdomains with mutual overlap conditions
$w_k + w_j = 1$ in the overlap; with several partners $d_j$ is the sum
over the partners containing $~x$ and $d_k$ the minimum over the own
overlap side sets. An overlap in which $d_k + d_j$ is below $10^{-3}$ of
the element size at some point (a zero-width overlap) aborts the run. The
weights are evaluated once and cached. Nonoverlapping and contact
decompositions take unit weights, so their total is the plain sum. The
weighted stored and kinetic energies, the latter in the staggered form for
central difference, are written at every stop as described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:output}, and reduce to the energy of the undecomposed body
at Schwarz convergence.

\section{The classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange}
\label{sec:classical}

Two subdomains share an interface $\Gamma$ given on each side as a side
set. The side whose \code{default BC type} is \code{Dirichlet} receives the
partner's kinematic fields and the other, \code{Neumann}, receives the
partner's internal force; the two values must differ. With
\code{swap BC types: true} on both sides the roles are exchanged once per
stop, before its Schwarz iteration.

\paragraph{Dirichlet side.} With $~P_D$ the Dirichlet projector
\eqref{eq:dirichlet-projector} of side $D$ and the partner fields
interpolated in time, relaxed as in Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation},
\begin{equation}
  ~u_D|_\Gamma = ~P_D\, ~u_N, \qquad
  ~v_D|_\Gamma = ~P_D\, ~v_N, \qquad
  ~a_D|_\Gamma = ~P_D\, ~a_N ,
  \label{eq:classical-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
all three prescribed and their degrees of freedom marked not free.

\paragraph{Neumann side.} With $~N_N$ the Neumann projector
\eqref{eq:neumann-projector} of side $N$, the force
\begin{equation}
  ~f_N = ~N_N \bigl( -\,~f^{\mathrm{int}}_D|_\Gamma \bigr)
  \label{eq:classical-neumann}
\end{equation}
is added to the boundary force of the interface nodes of $N$, with
$~f^{\mathrm{int}}_D$ interpolated in time from the partner's history. This
is the static reaction of the Dirichlet side; it omits the inertia of the
interface nodes and the loads applied on them, which Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}
measures to be of the size of the reaction itself in dynamics.

\paragraph{Relaxation.} Under fixed and recursive Aitken relaxation the
three partner fields are blended with the same factor, formed from the
partner's whole displacement field. Under secant Aitken relaxation the
displacement is blended and the velocity and acceleration are recovered
from the partner's Newmark predictors by \eqref{eq:newmark-from-u} when
the partner is a Newmark subdomain, and left unrelaxed otherwise. Under
Anderson acceleration the three fields have separate histories and share
the coefficients formed from the projected displacement residual.

\paragraph{Diagnostics and stopping.} At every Schwarz iteration the log
reports, for each Dirichlet--Neumann pair, the one-sided jumps of the
velocity and the displacement and the force residual defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping}; for the classical exchange they are
reported only. The iteration stops by the update criterion of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping}, and the initial acceleration of each subdomain
is computed per subdomain with the interface rows excluded from the
correction (Sec.~\ref{sec:initialization}).

\subsection{Interface predictor}
\label{sec:predictor}

With \code{interface predictor: true} the first-listed subdomain of a
nonoverlapping pair (Dirichlet--Neumann or Robin--Robin) reads at
iteration $0$ a predictor of the partner's state instead of the state at
$t_{s-1}$. For a quasi-static pair each side extrapolates its interface
displacement, $~u^{\mathrm{pred}} = 2 ~u_s - ~u_{s-1}$, from the second
stop on. For a dynamic pair the predictor velocity is the velocity of a
perfectly inelastic collision of the two interface traces, with the row
sums of the boundary mass matrices as masses,
$~v^{\mathrm{pred}}_k = (~m_k ~v_k + ~P_k ~m_j \circ ~P_k ~v_j)/(~m_k +
~P_k ~m_j)$ on side $k$ and its projection on side $j$, the predictor
displacement is $~u_s + \Delta t_c\, ~v^{\mathrm{pred}}$, and the
acceleration is that of the stop. The predicted internal force of the
side whose force is read is $2 ~f_s - ~f_{s-1}$.

\paragraph{Restrictions.} The exchange does not conserve the energy of a
dynamic problem (Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}); the matching exchange is the
dynamic coupling.
\section{The matching Dirichlet--Neumann exchange}
\label{sec:matching}

The matching exchange is selected by \code{matching: velocity} or
\code{matching: displacement} on both sides of a \code{Schwarz DN
nonoverlap} pair, with the same value on both. It is called the matching
exchange because its Dirichlet side matches one kinematic quantity of the
partner, the velocity or, for trapezoidal pairs, the displacement, and
derives the other two from its own time integrator; the coupled problem it
solves is the constrained problem of Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained}, whose
constraint is that matching and whose multiplier is the interface force.
It differs from the classical exchange in five respects: the Neumann side
receives the full dynamic (d'Alembert) reaction of its partner; both
transfers are built from one cross-mass matrix, so that the force transfer
is the transpose of the kinematic transfer; the Dirichlet side receives
one quantity only; the iteration stops on the interface jump and the force
residual; and the initial acceleration is computed on the coupled system.

\subsection{The constrained coupled problem}
\label{sec:constrained}

The coupled problem consists of \eqref{eq:motion} on both sides with one
interface force acting in opposite directions,
\begin{equation}
  ~\lambda_D = ~\lambda, \qquad ~\lambda_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda ,
  \label{eq:action-reaction}
\end{equation}
and the velocity constraint at the end of every window,
\begin{equation}
  ~v_D = \Pi_D\, ~v_N \quad \text{on } \Gamma .
  \label{eq:velocity-constraint}
\end{equation}
The interface force $~\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier of
\eqref{eq:velocity-constraint}. On conforming meshes $\Pi_D$ is the identity
on the interface nodes and the formulation is that of Gravouil and
Combescure~\cite{GravouilCombescure2001}, a dual Schur formulation with
velocity continuity. Prakash and Hjelmstad~\cite{PrakashHjelmstad2004}
impose the constraint only at the end of a coarse step, with the force on
the fine side interpolated linearly in time; Connors, Owen, Kuberry, and
Bochev~\cite{ConnorsEtAl2024} cast the same structure as a
discontinuous-Galerkin-in-time multirate framework whose lowest flux order
is this scheme. All three assume the same spatial discretization on both
sides of the interface, so that their interface map is Boolean; the
matching exchange removes that assumption, with the $L^2$ projector
$\Pi_D$ in place of the Boolean map, and the identities of
Sec.~\ref{sec:identity} hold for any cross-mass matrix $~B$.

The force on the interface rows of side $D$ is, by \eqref{eq:motion}, its
d'Alembert reaction
\begin{equation}
  ~r_D = ~C_D \bigl( ~M_D\, ~a_D + ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_D - ~f^{\mathrm{body}}_D
        - ~f^{\mathrm{bdry}}_D \bigr) = ~\lambda ,
  \label{eq:reaction}
\end{equation}
with $~f^{\mathrm{body}}_D$ the body force and $~f^{\mathrm{bdry}}_D$ the
other boundary loads acting on the interface rows. The reaction includes
the inertia of the interface nodes; the static reaction
$-~f^{\mathrm{int}}$ omits a term of the size of the reaction itself
(Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}). The mass in \eqref{eq:reaction} is the
consistent mass for a Newmark side and the lumped mass for a central
difference side.

\subsection{The energy identity}
\label{sec:identity}

Connors et al.\ (their Eq.~(102)) give the energy balance of one side. For
side $k$ advanced by $m_k$ Newmark steps with a linear stiffness, an
interface force $~f_k$ on its interface rows, and no other loads,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{E}_{k}^{m_k} - \tilde{E}_{k}^{0}
  + \Bigl(\gamma_k - \tfrac12\Bigr) \sum_{n=1}^{m_k}
    (\Delta ~a_k^n)^T ~S_k\, \Delta ~a_k^n
  = \frac{1}{\Delta t_k} \sum_{n=1}^{m_k}
    (\Delta ~v_k^n)^T ~C_k^T\, \Delta ~f_k^n ,
  \label{eq:per-side}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta(\cdot)^n = (\cdot)^n - (\cdot)^{n-1}$ and $\tilde{E}_k$ the
term of side $k$ in \eqref{eq:rate-invariant}. For $~S_k$ positive
definite the second term has the sign of $\gamma_k - 1/2$: an uncoupled
side ($~f_k = 0$) dissipates $\tilde{E}_k$ for $\gamma_k > 1/2$, conserves
it for $\gamma_k = 1/2$, and increases it at every step for
$\gamma_k < 1/2$, which is the instability of the Newmark scheme below
$\gamma = 1/2$. With $\gamma_k = 1/2$ the second term vanishes.

\begin{proposition}[Equal steps]
\label{prop:equal}
Let both sides take one step $\Delta t$ per window, with $\gamma = 1/2$ on
both, a linear stiffness, no loads on the interface rows other than the
interface force, $~f_D = ~\lambda$ and $~f_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda$, and
$~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ at every step. Then $\tilde{E}_D + \tilde{E}_N$ is
constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:per-side} the change over one step is
$\Delta t^{-1}\bigl[(\Delta ~v_D)^T \Delta~\lambda
- (\Delta ~v_N)^T \Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda\bigr]
= \Delta t^{-1} (\Delta ~v_D - \Pi_D \Delta ~v_N)^T \Delta~\lambda = 0$.
\end{proof}

The cancellation needs only the transposed force transfer and the
one-sided constraint; it holds for any cross-mass matrix $~B$, so for any
mesh ratio, and for any pair of Newmark schemes with $\gamma = 1/2$. It is
the interface work identity of Gravouil and Combescure (their Eq.~(49))
written with $\Pi_D$ in place of the Boolean interface map. Identical
integrators at equal steps conserve $E$ as well: the constraint and the
common Newmark relations then give $~u_D = \Pi_D ~u_N$ up to the initial
data, and the interface power $~\lambda^T (~v_D - \Pi_D ~v_N)$ vanishes at
the end of every step.

\begin{proposition}[Different steps]
\label{prop:subcycled}
Let side $D$ take one step $\Delta T$ per window and side $N$ take $m$
steps $\Delta T/m$, under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:equal}
except that $~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ holds only at the two ends of the window,
and let the force on $N$ at its substep $j$ be the linear interpolation
\begin{equation}
  ~f_N^{\,j} = -\Pi_D^T \Bigl[\bigl(1 - \tfrac{j}{m}\bigr)~\lambda^0
             + \tfrac{j}{m}\, ~\lambda^1\Bigr], \qquad j = 0, \dots, m,
  \label{eq:interpolated-force}
\end{equation}
of the forces $~\lambda^0$ and $~\lambda^1$ on $D$ at the window ends. Then
$\tilde{E}_D + \tilde{E}_N$ is constant over the window.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Delta ~f_N^j = -\Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda / m$ for every substep, with
$\Delta~\lambda = ~\lambda^1 - ~\lambda^0$, so the right side of
\eqref{eq:per-side} for $N$ is
$\frac{m}{\Delta T}\sum_{j=1}^{m} (\Delta ~v_N^j)^T(-\Pi_D^T \Delta~\lambda/m)
= -\Delta T^{-1}\bigl(\Pi_D(~v_N^m - ~v_N^0)\bigr)^T \Delta~\lambda$, and
that of $D$ is $\Delta T^{-1}(~v_D^1 - ~v_D^0)^T \Delta~\lambda$. The two
cancel when $~v_D = \Pi_D ~v_N$ at both window ends.
\end{proof}

This is Eq.~(103) of Connors et al.\ with $\Pi_D$ in place of their Boolean
map, their condition (108) at flux order $r = 1$, and Eq.~(70) of Prakash
and Hjelmstad. The force on $N$ at the window start must be the converged
force of the previous window, and the loads on the interface rows of $D$
must be constant within the window, because the reaction
\eqref{eq:reaction} is formed from their current value.

\paragraph{The coarse step must be the Dirichlet side.} The same sums
cancel when the fine-step side is the Dirichlet side and meets the
linearly interpolated coarse velocity at every substep, the arrangement of
Gravouil and Combescure. That arrangement conserves $\tilde{E}$ but lacks
the flux projection property of Connors et al.\ (their Eq.~(80)): the
impulse that the fine side receives over the window, the trapezoidal sum of
its reactions at every substep, differs from the impulse that the coarse
side receives, the trapezoid of the two end values. The interface impulse
residual of a window,
\begin{equation}
  ~\rho = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{\Delta T}{2m}\bigl(~f_N^{\,j-1} + ~f_N^{\,j}\bigr)
        + \Pi_D^T\, \frac{\Delta T}{2}\bigl(~\lambda^0 + ~\lambda^1\bigr),
  \label{eq:impulse}
\end{equation}
vanishes identically in the coarse-step arrangement. In the fine-step
arrangement it is of the order of the impulse itself, and the energy $E$
varies by $12$ to $51\%$ over the period of the first bending mode of the
beam while $\tilde{E}$ is conserved (Sec.~\ref{sec:verify-subcycled}).
$\tilde{E}$ weights the lowest mode least, so it does not detect the
variation. The code does not change the assignment, which is the input's;
since October 16, 2026 it warns at setup when the Dirichlet side has the
finer step, is the explicit member of a mixed pair, or has fewer interface
nodes than its partner with the same integrator and step
(Sec.~\ref{sec:roles}).

\paragraph{Scope.} Propositions~\ref{prop:equal} and~\ref{prop:subcycled}
hold for a linear stiffness. For a nonlinear material the exchange still
satisfies the velocity constraint exactly and does no interface work at the
fixed point;
the energy then varies by the time-step error of the subdomain integrators
themselves. For central difference that error is of order $\Delta t^2$ and
does not grow: the scheme conserves a modified energy that differs from $E$
by a state-dependent term of order $\Delta t^2$
\cite{HairerLubichWanner2006}. Conservation of $E$ to roundoff for a
nonlinear material requires an energy-momentum
integrator~\cite{SimoTarnow1992,Gonzalez1996} in each subdomain and a
midpoint velocity constraint in the exchange, which the code does not
have.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:matching-setup}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Assign the Dirichlet role (Sec.~\ref{sec:roles}) with
  \code{default BC type}.
\item Assemble $~B$, $~W_D$, and $~W_N$ of \eqref{eq:cross-mass} on the
  reference configuration, $~B$ with the facet quadrature of the Dirichlet
  side, and form $\Pi_D$ of \eqref{eq:projector}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:mass-matrices}). Check that $\Pi_D$ reproduces constants
  (the partition-of-unity error $\max |\Pi_D \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}|$ is at
  roundoff when the quadrature is exact).
\item Compute the initial acceleration on the coupled system
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:initial}).
\end{enumerate}
Both integrators must be Newmark or central difference without
HHT-$\alpha$. Displacement matching requires the same
Newmark parameters with $\beta > 0$ and the same step on both sides, and
aborts if the steps taken differ during the run; velocity matching admits
different steps and central difference. The exchange cannot be combined
with \code{swap BC types}.

\subsection{One window}
\label{sec:window}

Given the converged state at the window start and the force $~\lambda^0$ of
the previous window:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Initialize the datum $~q_D^\ast$ of the Dirichlet side, where $~q$
  is the matched quantity, with the projected partner value at the window
  start (the first iteration of a stop reads the partner's state at
  $t_{s-1}$, Sec.~\ref{sec:iteration}).
\item Repeat until the stopping criterion of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:matching-stopping} holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item Advance side $D$ over the window with $~q_D = ~q_D^\ast$ imposed on
    its interface nodes at the end of each of its substeps, the other two
    kinematic fields from \eqref{eq:newmark-from-v} (velocity matching) or
    \eqref{eq:newmark-from-u} (displacement matching) with its own
    $\beta$, $\gamma$, and substep, and form its reaction
    $~\lambda^1 = ~r_D$ by \eqref{eq:reaction}.
  \item Advance side $N$ over its substeps with the force
    \eqref{eq:interpolated-force} on its interface nodes (at equal steps,
    $~f_N = -\Pi_D^T ~\lambda^1$); in the code this is the time
    interpolation \eqref{eq:interp} of $D$'s acceleration and internal
    force history in \eqref{eq:reaction}, with $D$'s current body and
    boundary forces.
  \item Form the new datum $\Pi_D ~q_N$ at the window end, with $~q_N$
    interpolated in time to each substep of $D$ under subcycling, and relax
    it (Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}, with the Aitken factor and the Anderson
    coefficients formed from the projected trace) into $~q_D^\ast$.
  \end{enumerate}
\item Commit the window and keep $~\lambda^1$ as the next $~\lambda^0$.
\end{enumerate}
Each Schwarz iteration restores both sides to the state at the window
start before it advances them. The datum is imposed before the Newmark
predictor of the receiving substep, with the interface rows still holding
the substep-start state from which the predictors of
\eqref{eq:newmark-from-v} are formed; at $t = 0$, where no step is in
progress, the Dirichlet side imposes the three projected fields instead.
The velocity constraint is the general choice; the displacement
constraint, between the same trapezoidal scheme on both sides at equal
steps, gives the same solution to the Schwarz tolerance (test 136).

\subsection{Stopping criterion}
\label{sec:matching-stopping}

With $\norm{~x}^2_{~W} = \sum_c ~x_c^T ~W ~x_c$ over the three Cartesian
components, the Dirichlet side's one-sided relative jump of a quantity
$~q$ and the force residual are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\norm{~q_D - \Pi_D ~q_N}_{~W_D}}{\norm{~q_D}_{~W_D}},
  \qquad
  \frac{\norm{~f_N + \Pi_D^T ~r_D}_{~W_N^{-1}}}{\norm{\Pi_D^T ~r_D}_{~W_N^{-1}}},
  \label{eq:stopping}
\end{equation}
the discrete $L^2$ norms of the trace and of its dual, with $~f_N$ the
force applied on $N$ in the same iteration. The log reports the velocity
and displacement jumps and the force residual of every Dirichlet--Neumann
pair at every Schwarz iteration; for a matching pair each value is the
largest over the substeps of the window, the jumps at every substep of
$D$ against $N$'s history interpolated to that time, the force residual at
every substep of $N$ against $D$'s history. The iteration of a matching
pair stops when the jump of the matched quantity and the force residual
are both at or below $\epsilon$, the \code{matching relative tolerance},
or when the root mean square jump
$\norm{~q_D - \Pi_D ~q_N}_{~W_D} / (\mathbf{1}^T ~W_D \mathbf{1})^{1/2}$
is at or below the \code{matching absolute tolerance} (default $0$, no
absolute test), which is in the units of the matched quantity; a
displacement tolerance divided by the step is not a valid velocity floor
(on the cylinders of Sec.~\ref{sec:verification} it amounts to $200$\,m/s
and stops the iteration after one pass). When every coupling of the
simulation is a matching pair, this is the only stopping test, also at the
first iteration of a stop, and the update criterion of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping} is not used. Measured against the earlier rule that
also required the update to meet the controller's absolute
tolerance at the first iteration, this changes only the stops at which
nothing has yet reached the interface: on the nonconforming beam
($2000$ stops) the $7953$ Schwarz iterations and the energy output are
identical, and on the linear cylinders ($2000$ stops, fixed relaxation
$0.5$, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$) the first seven stops take $0$ iterations
instead of $1$, every later count of the $53\,694$ is the same, and the
energies of the two runs differ by at most $7.7\times 10^{-14}$ ($E$) and
$4.4\times 10^{-12}$ ($\tilde{E}$).

\paragraph{Stall.} Let $\mu_i$ be the larger of the jump and the force
residual, maximized over the matching pairs, at iteration $i$. The
iteration is stalled when $\mu_i$ has not fallen $5\%$ below its smallest
value for three consecutive counted iterations (every iteration when all
couplings are matching pairs; otherwise only those at which the
update criterion holds). The residual is not monotone under
Aitken or Anderson acceleration, so a single non-decreasing iteration is
not a stall. A stalled stop is accepted with a warning and the Anderson
history cleared (\code{stalled interface jump action: warn}, the default)
or aborts the run (\code{abort}). Every stopping tolerance leaves a drift
of the conserved quantity of the order of $\epsilon$ per window,
accumulated over the windows of the run (Sec.~\ref{sec:verify-relaxation}).

\subsection{Coupled initial acceleration}
\label{sec:initial}

The initial accelerations must satisfy the coupled problem at $t = 0$,
including the acceleration constraint $~a_D = \Pi_D ~a_N$, or the first
step changes $\tilde{E}$ by $0.2$ to $0.9\%$ and, for an explicit pair, the
difference of the two interface accelerations alternates in sign at every
step without decaying. The coupled initial acceleration is a Schwarz
iteration at $t = 0$ in which $D$ imposes the projected partner
displacement, velocity, and acceleration, $N$ receives the d'Alembert
reaction of $D$, and each side solves for its acceleration with the
coupling rows included in the correction of Sec.~\ref{sec:setting}. The
imposed interface acceleration is relaxed with
the fixed factor, with the Aitken factor of the selected form,
\eqref{eq:aitken-recursive} or \eqref{eq:aitken-secant}, or by Anderson
acceleration, as the input selects. The iteration stops when the one-sided acceleration jump and the
force residual of every pair are at or below $\epsilon$, or after
\code{maximum iterations} with a warning. It converges in $2$ iterations
on conforming meshes with identical integrators, $16$ to $24$ on
nonconforming meshes, and about $30$ for mixed pairs at a fixed
relaxation factor of $0.5$, and in $2$ to $9$ with Anderson acceleration.
For two explicit sides the direct solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:direct} gives it
in one step.

\subsection{Assignment of the Dirichlet role}
\label{sec:roles}

The linearized gain of one Schwarz iteration at fixed relaxation is
\begin{equation}
  ~G = -\,\Pi_D\, ~H_N\, \Pi_D^T\, ~S_D ,
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
with $~H_N = ~C_N \tilde{~M}_N^{-1} ~C_N^T$ the interface flexibility of $N$
($\tilde{~M} = ~M + \beta \Delta t^2 ~K$, the lumped mass for central
difference) and $~S_D$ the interface dynamic stiffness of $D$. A fixed
relaxation factor $\theta$ converges if and only if the spectral radius of
$(1 - \theta)~I + \theta ~G$ is below one; for a real spectrum in $[a, b]$
the optimal factor is $\theta^\ast = 2/((1-a) + (1-b))$ with contraction
$(b - a)/((1-a) + (1-b))$. Table~\ref{tab:dn-gain} lists the computed
eigenvalues on the beam. The rules that follow, in order of precedence:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item With different time steps, the coarse-step side is the Dirichlet side
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:subcycled} and the impulse residual).
\item With different integrators, the implicit side is the Dirichlet side:
  with the explicit side as the Dirichlet side the eigenvalues reach
  $-13.3$ and every factor $\theta \ge 0.5$ diverges.
\item With the same integrator on a nonconforming interface, the finer mesh
  is the Dirichlet side: the eigenvalues then lie in $[g, 0]$ with
  $|g| \le 1$; with the coarser mesh as the Dirichlet side they reach $-8$
  for an explicit pair.
\end{enumerate}
When rules 1 and 3 conflict (a finer mesh with a finer step), giving the
finer mesh the coarser step satisfies both where the stability limit of an
explicit side allows it. With Anderson acceleration every assignment
measured converges, so the rules then set the iteration count, not the
feasibility.

\subsection{Direct interface solve for two explicit subdomains}
\label{sec:direct}

For central difference on both sides at equal steps, with velocity
matching, the iteration can be replaced by one solve per step
(\code{interface solve: direct} on both sides; every coupling of the
simulation must then be such a pair). Central difference computes
$~u^{n+1}$ before the acceleration, and the internal force at $~u^{n+1}$
does not depend on the interface force. Advancing both sides with free
interface rows and no interface force gives the velocities
$~v^{\mathrm{free}}$; the interface force adds
$\gamma \Delta t\, ~m_D^{-1}~\lambda$ to the velocity of $D$ and
$-\gamma\Delta t\, ~m_N^{-1}\Pi_D^T ~\lambda$ to that of $N$, with $~m_D$,
$~m_N$ the lumped masses of the interface rows. The velocity constraint
then gives, per Cartesian component,
\begin{equation}
  \gamma \Delta t \bigl(~m_D^{-1} + \Pi_D\, ~m_N^{-1}\, \Pi_D^T\bigr)
  ~\lambda = \Pi_D ~v_N^{\mathrm{free}} - ~v_D^{\mathrm{free}} .
  \label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
The matrix is symmetric positive definite, of the size of the Dirichlet
trace, and constant while the lumped masses and $\Pi_D$ are; rows held by
other Dirichlet conditions take a zero inverse mass, and a row held on
both sides makes the matrix singular and aborts. It is the interface
operator $~H = \sum_k \gamma_k \Delta t_k ~C_k \tilde{~M}_k^{-1} ~C_k^T$ of
Gravouil and Combescure for two lumped-mass sides, written on the Dirichlet
trace. It is factored once by a dense Cholesky factorization ($3920$
unknowns per component on the cylinders, $0.7$\,s), and at every step both
sides are advanced, \eqref{eq:direct} is solved, and the interface
accelerations and velocities are corrected by $~m_D^{-1} ~\lambda$ and
$\gamma \Delta t\, ~m_D^{-1} ~\lambda$ on $D$, and by the same with
$-\Pi_D^T ~\lambda$ and $~m_N$ on $N$; the displacements are not
corrected, because central difference has already formed them. The solve
is exact for nonlinear materials, because the internal force is evaluated
at the predicted displacement. The same matrix gives the coupled initial
acceleration from $~a_D = \Pi_D ~a_N$. Relaxation and the stopping
tolerances do not apply; the stop takes no Schwarz iteration.

\subsection{Requirements on a subdomain code}
\label{sec:integration}

The exchange requires of each subdomain code, beyond a standard step:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item a step with a prescribed velocity (or displacement) on a set of
  boundary nodes, with the other two kinematic fields of those nodes from
  \eqref{eq:newmark-from-v} or \eqref{eq:newmark-from-u} (the Dirichlet
  side);
\item a step with a prescribed nodal force on a set of boundary nodes,
  constant or linear in time over the window (the Neumann side);
\item the nodal d'Alembert reaction \eqref{eq:reaction} on a set of
  boundary nodes after a step;
\item saving and restoring the state at the window start;
\item the boundary mass matrix and the facet quadrature of its interface,
  to assemble $~B$ (performed once, by either code or by the coupler).
\end{enumerate}
An implicit code provides items 1 to 3 as a Newton solve at fixed boundary
data; an explicit code provides them as a division by the lumped mass. The
coupler itself holds $\Pi_D$, the relaxation state, and, for two explicit
sides, the factorization of \eqref{eq:direct}. The other couplings of this
note require subsets of the same operations: the overlapping coupling
items 1 (with all three fields prescribed) and 4 and a point location in
the partner mesh; the classical exchange items 1, 2, 4, and 5 and the
internal force on the interface nodes; the Robin--Robin condition a step
with the term $\alpha ~W ~u$ and its tangent added to the residual.

\section{The Robin--Robin condition}
\label{sec:rr}

Two subdomains share an interface. Each side $k$ imposes the Robin
condition $~t_k + \alpha_k ~W_k ~u_k = ~g_k$ on its interface rows, with
$~t_k$ its interface traction, $~W_k$ its boundary mass matrix, and
$\alpha_k > 0$ its \code{robin parameter} (required; the two sides may
differ). The datum is formed from the partner's traction and displacement,
\begin{equation}
  ~g_k = ~N_k \bigl( -\,~f^{\mathrm{int}}_j|_\Gamma \bigr)
       + \alpha_k ~W_k\, ~P_k\, ~u_j ,
  \label{eq:robin-datum}
\end{equation}
with $~P_k$ and $~N_k$ the Dirichlet and Neumann projectors
\eqref{eq:dirichlet-projector} and \eqref{eq:neumann-projector} of side
$k$, each side building its own, and the partner fields interpolated in
time. The partner's internal force is its elastic internal force; the
partner's own Robin term is not part of it. The datum is added to the
boundary force of the interface nodes of $k$; the self term
$\alpha_k ~W_k ~u_k|_\Gamma$ is added to the internal force at every
residual evaluation, including the initial-acceleration solves, and its
tangent $\alpha_k ~W_k$ is added to the Hessian of implicit solvers. The
condition solved on side $k$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
  ~f^{\mathrm{int}}_k(~u_k) + \alpha_k ~W_k ~u_k|_\Gamma - ~f^{\mathrm{ext}}_k
  = ~N_k \bigl( -\,~f^{\mathrm{int}}_j|_\Gamma \bigr) + \alpha_k ~W_k ~P_k ~u_j .
  \label{eq:robin-condition}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Relaxation.} The side listed later in \code{domains} relaxes
its datum: $~\lambda_i = (1 - \theta_i)\, ~\lambda_{i-1} + \theta_i\, ~g_i$,
with the fixed, Aitken, or Anderson factor of Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}
formed from the datum itself, and $~\lambda_{-1} = 0$ at a new slot, so
that the first iteration of every stop applies $\theta_0 ~g_0$ on that
side. The earlier-listed side applies $~g_i$ unrelaxed.

\paragraph{Initialization and stopping.} When an interface node has a
nonzero initial displacement or velocity, the initialization runs the two
refinement passes of Sec.~\ref{sec:initialization}. The iteration stops by
the update criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping}. The condition is not
absorbing: under a dynamic integrator it can increase the energy, and the code
warns when a Robin--Robin side is not quasi-static. It is the coupling of
the quasi-static examples; no impedance (velocity-proportional) term is
implemented.

\section{The contact coupling}
\label{sec:contact}

Two bodies whose surfaces, given as a side set on each, may come into
contact are coupled by a Dirichlet--Neumann exchange that is active while
contact is detected. Each side names its own side set, the partner, and
the partner's side set, and a \code{friction type}: \code{frictionless}
or \code{tied}. The side of the subdomain listed first in \code{domains}
is the Dirichlet side; \code{swap BC types: true} exchanges the roles at
every stop in which contact is active. The transfer operators are the
Dirichlet and Neumann projectors of Sec.~\ref{sec:mass-matrices}, built
once at initialization on the reference configuration.

\paragraph{Detection.} One flag holds the contact state of the whole
simulation. It is evaluated after every stop has been solved, and at
initialization. While contact is inactive, the stop is solved with the
subdomains advanced independently and the contact surfaces traction-free,
and contact becomes active when any node $~x$ of the side set of a
contact condition, in the current configuration, has a closest-point
projection $~y$ onto a partner facet, within a search radius of ten times
the smallest nodal spacing of its own side set, that lies inside the facet
and on the far side of it: the signed distance
$-\,\mathrm{sign}((~y - ~x) \cdot ~n)\, \norm{~y - ~x} \le 0$, with $~n$ the
facet normal. While contact is active, the stop is solved by the Schwarz
iteration, and contact persists while any node of a contact side set has
a compressive normal force, $~f_i \cdot ~n_i \le 0$, with $~f_i$ the force
transferred from the partner and $~n_i$ the nodal normal of the side set.
When the state changes after a stop, the stop is restored and solved once
more in the new state, without a further check. The state is written to
\code{contact-<stop>.csv} at the CSV output stops.

\paragraph{Dirichlet side.} With $~P_D$ the Dirichlet projector, the
transferred values are the projected current position
$\hat{~x} = ~P_D(~X_N + ~u_N)$, velocity $\hat{~v} = ~P_D ~v_N$, and
acceleration $\hat{~a} = ~P_D ~a_N$ of the partner, relaxed as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:classical}. A tied condition prescribes
$~u_i = \hat{~x}_i - ~X_i$, $~v_i = \hat{~v}_i$, $~a_i = \hat{~a}_i$ on every
node of the side set, all three components fixed. A frictionless
condition prescribes the normal component only,
\begin{equation}
  ~x_i \leftarrow (~I - ~n_i ~n_i^T)\, ~x_i + ~n_i ~n_i^T\, \hat{~x}_i,
  \qquad
  ~v_i \leftarrow (~I - ~n_i ~n_i^T)\, ~v_i + ~n_i ~n_i^T\, \hat{~v}_i,
  \qquad
  ~a_i \leftarrow (~I - ~n_i ~n_i^T)\, ~a_i + ~n_i ~n_i^T\, \hat{~a}_i,
  \label{eq:contact-frictionless}
\end{equation}
with $~n_i$ the normal of a facet of the side set containing node $i$, on
the current configuration for finite kinematics. The code requires the
normal to be along $\pm ~e_x$ to $10^{-6}$, fixes the $x$ component, and
leaves the $y$ and $z$ components free; a side set whose normal is not
along $x$ aborts.

\paragraph{Neumann side.} With $~N_N$ the Neumann projector, the force
$~f_N = ~N_N(-\,~f^{\mathrm{int}}_D|_\Gamma)$ of \eqref{eq:classical-neumann}
is added to the boundary force of the side set, in full for a tied
condition and as its normal component $~n_i ~n_i^T ~f_{N,i}$ for a
frictionless one. The reaction transmitted is the static one, as in the
classical exchange.

\paragraph{Options and restrictions.} \code{naive stabilized: true} sets
the acceleration of every node of every contact side set to zero after
each substep while contact is active. The exchange shares the relaxation
of the classical exchange and does not use the interface predictor. There
is no friction law: the two options are no tangential transfer and a full
tie. The contact state is one flag for all pairs of the simulation, and
when it is active every node of the Dirichlet side set is constrained,
whether or not it is in contact with the partner; there is no nodal active set, gap
function, or inequality enforcement. The surfaces must be declared in
advance as side-set pairs; there is no global search and no self-contact.
The projectors are not rebuilt during the run, so sliding is not tracked
by the transfer operators. A restart is rejected with contact. The
iteration stops by the update criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:stopping}.
\section{Verification of the matching exchange}
\label{sec:verification}

\subsection{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\paragraph{Cantilever beam.} A linear elastic bar ($E = 6.895$\,GPa,
$\nu = 0.25$, $\rho = 2768$\,kg/m$^3$; $254 \times 25.4 \times 12.7$\,mm),
clamped at $x = 0$ and released at rest from the bent shape
$u_y = 0.3937\,x^2$ (tip deflection $25.4$\,mm), with initial energy
$1506$\,J and a first bending period of about $10$\,ms. It is split at
$x = 127$\,mm into a clamped part and a free part. The free part is meshed
with HEX8 elements of $6.35$\,mm; the clamped part at $6.35$\,mm
(conforming), $8.47$\,mm (ratio $1{:}0.75$, facets not nested), or
$12.7$\,mm (ratio $1{:}0.5$, nested). Stops of $1\,\mu$s (or $0.5\,\mu$s
where stated), $10$\,ms. The first letter of an integrator pair is the
clamped part.

\paragraph{Nested cylinders.} A steel shell (outer radius $88.9$\,mm,
height $330$\,mm), a soft filler of one fiftieth of the shell's wave
impedance, and an offset core, all neohookean, launched at $-35$\,m/s
against a support that fixes the bottom face in $z$. The interface is a
closed surface inside the filler around the core (a lateral surface and two
caps); the inner mesh is $5.1$\,mm and the outer $6.35$\,mm (ratio
$1{:}0.8$, not nested), $17$ to $26$ thousand elements per subdomain.
Central difference on both sides at a Courant number of $0.5$, stops of
$0.5\,\mu$s, $2$\,ms. A linear elastic version with the same meshes
(filler $E = 0.214$\,GPa, $\nu = 0.494$) moves at $-5$\,m/s for $1$\,ms.
The inner side is the Dirichlet side.

\paragraph{Ground truth.} The undecomposed beam keeps $E$ and $\tilde{E}$
within $1.7\times 10^{-13}$ over $10$\,ms with the trapezoidal rule and
within $2.7\times 10^{-14}$ with central difference; the trapezoidal value
requires a direct linear solver and a small tolerance on the
initial-acceleration solve.

\subsection{Inexact subdomain solves}
\label{sec:inexact}

Every Schwarz iteration solves each subdomain to the Newton tolerance of
its solver, although only the converged Schwarz iterate is used. Inexact
Schwarz solves the subdomains to a large Newton tolerance and relies on
the Schwarz iteration, stopped at a small $\epsilon$, for the accuracy. Its effect was measured on
the beam of Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmarks} with a neohookean material of the
same moduli, so that Newton takes more than one iteration per solve: two
trapezoidal subdomains, the matching exchange with Anderson
acceleration, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$, $10^4$ stops of $1\,\mu$s on the
mesh of level $1$ ($180$ elements) and $2\times 10^4$ stops of
$0.5\,\mu$s on the mesh of level $2$ ($1440$ elements). The reference
run has the relative Newton tolerance $10^{-12}$; the error of a run is
$\lVert u - u_{\mathrm{ref}} \rVert_2 / \lVert u_{\mathrm{ref}} \rVert_2$
over all nodes at $10$\,ms. Table~\ref{tab:inexact} gives the Newton
iterations of each run relative to the reference, the Schwarz iterations per
stop, and the error, for constant Newton tolerances, for one Newton
iteration per solve carried forward unconverged, and for the tolerance
ratio $\eta$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:inexact-newton}.

A constant Newton tolerance does not change the Schwarz iteration count,
and its error equals the Newton error, not the Schwarz stopping error. The
reason is the structure of the iteration: every Schwarz iteration restarts
the subdomain solves from the state at the start of the stop, not from the
previous Schwarz iterate, so with a fixed Newton setting each Schwarz
iteration applies the same map and the iteration converges to the fixed
point of that map, which carries the Newton error. The Schwarz criterion
measures the change between iterates and does not detect it: with one Newton
iteration per solve the iteration meets $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$ in $8$
iterations per stop and the result is wrong by $0.5\%$. The tolerance
ratio removes the error at $0.72$ to $0.74$ of the Newton iterations of the
reference. The larger ratio, $\eta = 0.1$, raises the Schwarz count by
$26$ to $28\%$ and removes most of the saving. The \code{tight final solve}
option, which
accepts Schwarz convergence only at an iteration whose Newton tolerance was
$10^{-12}$, was triggered at $50$ of the $10^4$ stops with $\eta = 0.01$
and did not reduce the error. On a machine with no other load the elapsed
time of the level $1$ runs relative to the reference ($1081$\,s on $4$ threads)
is $0.49$ for one Newton iteration per solve, $0.74$ for the tolerance
$10^{-4}$, and $0.81$ for $\eta = 0.01$, within $2.4\%$ of the ratios of
residual and tangent assemblies. The energy of the neohookean beam under
the trapezoidal rule falls by $5.0\%$ over the $10$\,ms at both levels,
identically in the monolithic run; the loss is divided by $3.6$ to $3.9$
at each halving of the step on a fixed mesh, so it is the second-order
error of the integrator at the Courant number the levels share, and the
energy is not a measure of the coupling for this material.

On a static problem the result differs. For a neohookean inclusion in a
neohookean matrix (\code{examples/nonoverlap/static-same-step/matrix-inclusion}
at $5\%$ strain in $10$ load steps, the classical exchange with Aitken
secant relaxation, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$) Newton tolerances from $10^{-6}$
to $10^{-10}$ save $15\%$ of the Newton iterations at an error of
$3\times 10^{-13}$, whereas $10^{-4}$ and larger raise the Schwarz count
from $36$ to $84$ or more per load step and reach the maximum of $128$, and the
tolerance ratio $\eta = 0.1$ saves $24\%$ at an error of
$6\times 10^{-13}$. A warm-started form of inexact Schwarz, in which each
subdomain solve starts from its previous Schwarz iterate so that one
Newton iteration per Schwarz iteration converges to the solution of the
coupled problem, is not implemented; it requires the predictor state
of the integrator to be carried across Schwarz iterations.

\subsection{The classical exchange}
\label{sec:baseline}

The classical Dirichlet--Neumann exchange of Sec.~\ref{sec:classical}
departs from the constrained problem in the five respects listed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:matching}: it transmits the static reaction
$-~f^{\mathrm{int}}$, builds its two transfers independently, imposes
displacement, velocity, and acceleration together, stops on the
change of $~u + \Delta t_c\, ~v$ over the whole domain, and computes the initial
acceleration per subdomain with the interface held fixed. The relative
difference between the force it applies on the Neumann side and the
transposed d'Alembert reaction of the Dirichlet side, measured in the
$~W_N^{-1}$ norm at the end of every stop, has the median $0.83$ (maximum
$4.2$) with explicit subdomains and $1.47$ (maximum $4.6$) with implicit
ones; under the matching exchange it is below $4\times 10^{-16}$. On the
beam the classical exchange inverts an element within $1$\,ms with implicit
subdomains on every mesh ratio and with $4{:}1$ steps, and with explicit
subdomains the energy varies by $2.6$ to $4.3\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:beam}). On
the neohookean cylinders it stays within $3\%$ over $2$\,ms with explicit
subdomains.

\section{Limitations and open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Schwarz stopping error.} Every iterated run stops at a
  tolerance, and the stopping error accumulates: the conserved quantity
  drifts by about $10^{-10}$ over $10^4$ stops at $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$, and
  by about $10^{-9}$ with the fine-step side as the Dirichlet side. How the
  drift scales with $\epsilon$ and with the number of windows, and whether
  the criterion \eqref{eq:stopping} needs refinement, has not been
  measured.
\item \emph{Warm start of the subdomain solves.} Each Schwarz iteration
  restarts the subdomain solves from the state at the start of the stop
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:inexact}), which bounds the saving of inexact
  subdomain solves at a fixed accuracy to the fraction of Newton
  iterations beyond the second. Starting each solve from the previous
  Schwarz iterate would let one Newton iteration per Schwarz iteration
  converge to the coupled solution; its effect on the Schwarz count and on
  the rate invariant identity has not been measured.
\item \emph{Mixed pair with an explicit coarse-step side.} The coarse-step
  side must be the Dirichlet side and an explicit Dirichlet side has a gain
  down to $-13$; Anderson acceleration converges at equal steps in this
  configuration but has not been measured with subcycling.
\item \emph{Cause of the energy variation with the fine-step side as the
  Dirichlet side.} The variation coincides with an impulse residual of the
  order of the impulse; that the residual causes it has not been tested
  separately.
\item \emph{Nonlinear materials.} Conservation of $E$ to roundoff needs an
  energy-momentum integrator and a midpoint velocity constraint.
\item \emph{Higher flux order and window solves.} Flux order $r \ge 2$ in
  the sense of Connors et al.\ and a direct interface solve over a
  subcycled window are not needed for conservation; whether they reduce
  the iteration count or the elapsed time has not been measured.
\item \emph{Implicit subdomains on the cylinders} and more than two
  subdomains have not been run with the matching exchange.
\item \emph{A conserving Robin condition.} A Robin condition assembled from
  the d'Alembert reaction and the one-sided constraint of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:constrained} has the fixed point of the constrained problem
  and could serve as a solver of it; it has not been implemented.
\item \emph{Contact.} The contact coupling has one active flag for the
  whole simulation, no nodal active set, no friction law, and transfer
  operators fixed at initialization (Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}).
\end{enumerate}
